Grading, source pixels, and what would falsify this

The grade measures the shaped result. Body overlap is the number of occupied columns shared by the upper and lower sections on the same seam row. Edge step is the larger difference between their left or right alpha edges. Split body pixels are those outside the largest four-connected component. Fail when step > 2 px, overlap < 9 px, split > 2 px, or the cape covers ≥ 12 new walking-body pixels below the seam. Warn when step = 2 px, overlap = 9 px, split > 0 px, or the cape covers 1–11 new pixels. Pass otherwise. These are inspection thresholds for this sprite.

Alpha > 0 counts as occupied. For each phase I mark the hip center by hand, keep the walk pixels within a hip-to-leg window above row 53, and keep the full walk silhouette from row 53 down. The punch cut maps to the same displayed seam after integer translation. The fixed cut uses that same displayed seam and keeps all pixels of each clip on its side of the cut. Equipment is drawn in fixture slot order. Sword, vest, hair, helmet and cape use the action frame intact in the shaped view. The cape metric counts occupied cape pixels where the shaped body gains coverage over the shifted original punch body. The overlay in the gear view marks those pixels in pink. It cannot score holes in cape art or judge a pose’s anatomy.

Same seam · high confidence in the geometric improvement. Look for a second hand, a chopped limb, or a flickering hip at a passing cell. Any of those would overturn a visual pass.
