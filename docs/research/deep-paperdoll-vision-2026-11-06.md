## What the current gates miss

1. **A blade can reverse around a fixed wrist and pass G9.** In a 64 × 64, three-cell walking control, the right wrist stays at (31, 30), the elbow at (35, 30), and a 12 × 3 solid blade begins at the wrist. Frames 0 and 2 extend right and frame 1 extends left. Both principal axes are 0 radians, both areas are 36, and the centroid-aligned IoU is 1. The wrist-space IoU is 3/69 = 0.043. Production `itemQa` returned `pass` with zero warnings. The unsigned PCA representation and centroid alignment both discard polarity. Current QA already has wrist-space occlusion evidence, but calls it only after a shape or angle difference triggers. [Equipment QA and held visibility](../../app/src/lib/items/items.ts)
2. **A coarse polarity heuristic needs a visibility control.** An offline prototype signed the PCA axis by the wrist-to-centroid projection, required projection magnitude ≥4 px, along-axis span ≥9 px, forearm length ≥3 px, and a centroid direction within 45° of the signed axis. It reported adjacent relative-forearm changes above 120°. Among retained idle/walk frames, eligibility was 24/24 for sword, 22/24 for axe, and 5/24 for bow. Across 47 eligible cyclic pairs, it reported one bow transition, east walk 0→1 (125°). The masks switch which side of the wrist contains most bow pixels. The two native frames also differ in occlusion and shape. The proxy alone cannot establish the intended bow orientation. Qualify on genuine one-ended blades separately from bows, double-ended weapons, near-round shields, and occlusion. Use warning-only evidence with the two exact frames before considering a stronger gate. [Existing weapon calibration and recovered grip controls](2026-11-02-weapon-aware-grip.md)
3. **A one-frame color pop can vanish in the average.** Eight 25-pixel synthetic apparel frames use the same mask and accepted palette except one frame with a 100-level red-channel shift over all 25 pixels. The per-frame distance to the approved design is 100. Clip distance is 12.5, below the G6 threshold of 15, and the dressed design comparison yields no warning. A max alone would overweight tiny glints. Pair local support with a per-frame distance and a same-material reference, then show the outlier only when the area and color change are meaningful. A material pixel permutation can also preserve the full palette histogram while moving or reversing a design motif. [G6 design comparison and color distances](../../app/src/lib/items/items.ts) · [Existing bounded material correction](../../app/src/lib/dress/item-palette.ts)
4. **Alpha probes must include the production decoder.** The frozen benchmark's 20 cases and 678 standing and posed cells contain zero fractional-alpha pixels in both raw output and final equipment layers. A direct synthetic `extractCell` call retains eight apparel pixels at alpha 180, but this bypasses `run.ts`'s `img` decoder, which applies `binaryAlpha` before production equipment evaluation. That direct probe does not establish a production fractional-alpha defect. The live trial evaluator now includes the same decoder policy while measuring raw alpha separately. Promoting a mixed edge can still reveal an RGB halo, so test fringe color and the resulting composite independently from alpha. [Production decoder](../../app/src/lib/items/run.ts#L331) · [Chroma matte](../../app/src/lib/image/chroma.ts) · [PNG and binary-alpha helpers](../../app/src/lib/image/png.ts)
5. **Offline recomposition could contradict the displayed item.** The original `recompose` used for M3 and benchmark contact sheets showed opaque body at all 986 cut pixels in the retained posed benchmark. The Studio renderer honors item-dependent `baseCut`. The diagnostic composite and the foreground/background split comparison now use the same cut operation. Generic callers can still pass only a body and attachment. The benchmark had zero labeled verdict misses before the fix, so the contact-sheet pixels needed a separate regression. [Benchmark and contact sheets](../../app/scripts/bench.ts) · [Runtime composition](../../app/src/lib/compose/compose.ts)

The earlier repaint probe supplies a separate counterexample for G2. Off-key skin classified as equipment leaves the protected-key denominator and can pass the gate. On a stencil-constrained repair, a forbidden repaint and an untrusted in-mask change are separate signals. Preserve raw QA and expose the distinction to review. Chroma scoring, exact comparison, and semantic ownership answer different questions. [Earlier 12-pixel repaint probe](tool-spikes-2026-11-06-chroma-and-occlusion.md) · [Submitted stencil tests](../../app/test/unit/item-stencil.test.ts)

## Proposed experiment order

1. **Make existing output auditable.** Correct the cut-blind diagnostic. Show body, approved same-facing reference, raw candidate, accepted layer, and the composited failure around a flagged cell. Where available, overlay protected pixels, final foreground and body cut as distinct marks. Use a warning to lead directly to the two frames at a grip or color transition. This uses retained artifacts and can ship independently of provider behavior.
2. **Add a defect set beside the current benchmark.** Keep exact gold outputs for protected eyes/hands, cropped weapons, rear/front hair, one-pixel color details, antialiased fringes, and mirrored views. Use synthetic mutations with known position, count, and owner to measure defect detection. Use real accepted and rejected output with hand-reviewed local labels to determine whether the same checks are useful on provider output. For temporal checks use actual playlist pairs and distinguish loop edges from play-once action semantics and absent partial-repair neighbors. Native output and nearest-neighbor zoom should use the same frame indices. [Earlier loop measurement](tool-spikes-2026-11-06-generation.md#offline-probe-2-loop-seam-and-false-positives) · [Frozen fixture benchmark](../testing/bench.md)
3. **Use local ownership to guide a single repair.** Define protected, confirmed equipment, and uncertain pixels around the affected body part. Project an approved mask from a same-facing reference into the measured target geometry, allowing a conservative extent for fabric and attachments. Use overlap from a sound previous and next frame to tighten the stencil only when both independently cover the defect. If an item disappears, input evidence is ambiguous, or the two sides disagree, keep the broad path and surface the reason. The inpaint request's permission mask remains distinct from the candidate's final equipment and depth labels. Compare output on the exact saved PNG and decode through production. [Current repair inputs and stencil enforcement](../../app/src/lib/items/run.ts)
4. **Pilot design anchors as a matched experiment.** Pick native 64–96 px samples from two different body silhouettes and independent facings. Include a rigid helmet with face-adjacent edges, a red or blue-green patterned tunic under a crossing arm, a long cape with a tucked pose, and a visibly asymmetric blade or one-handed axe. Pair current skeleton posing and frozen-frame inpaint by source design, pose, direction, key family, and prompt. Within the inpaint branch compare current broad permission with the restricted mask on the same target cells, and compare the standing reference with a previously accepted pose-similar same-view frame in a separate branch. Start with the smallest cases that distinguish the mechanisms. Record seeds, actual returned layout, the original mask and source image identities, route version, latency, and provider charges. Repeat across seeds and a second body before a default routing change. Retain every candidate and QA verdict to avoid selecting only favorable art.
5. **Qualify using decision dimensions.** The absolute contract is registered geometry, complete requested coverage, exact protected-body RGBA in the app composite, no added alpha outside authorized masks, correct mirror and pivot, and preview/export parity. Measure item false positives and misses separately by occlusion and material role. Measure color and landmark pop on jointly visible regions, silhouette/joint support in the target frame, frame-to-frame and actual loop transition changes, artist review and edits per accepted clip, and billable cost per accepted clip. Report per-slot and per-view distributions, worst cases, and human disagreements. Repeated provider requests establish stochastic reliability only for the sampled input family. Preserve frozen body ownership through review and exports.

Browser ONNX execution exists, with different WebGPU and WASM operator coverage. A new segmentation runtime would add model transfer, memory, client support, and evaluation work. Photographic checkpoints provide no demonstrated accuracy on native-scale sprite anatomy or two-pixel gear parts. Use published models as research controls and take the trimap, keyframe, and confidence ideas into the deterministic client. The configured PixelLab routes remain the production synthesis surface. The app already owns exact compositing, palette swaps, body hashes, and accepted-pixel export. ## How far can automatic dye matching go?

This offline challenge used 59 extracted standing views from 17 non-reference cases in the frozen benchmark. The original standing pixel is the exact target for each perturbed color. `autoRamps(colorCounts(standingViews))` supplies the item-level dye ownership labels for the experiment, while each view supplies its own candidate colors. The ownership labels are derived from the art by the current code. They are reproducible and are not independent artist annotations. Apply each of six RGB offsets separately to every visible pixel, preserving alpha and clipping at the channel limits. The offsets are ±(8, −4, 2), ±(2, 8, −4), and ±(−4, 2, 8). Multiply each offset by 2.5 for the stronger stress test. Each strength yields 138,822 perturbed owned-pixel observations and 13,230 perturbed unowned-pixel observations. These correlated observations characterize the replay. They do not estimate how often PixelLab makes each kind of shift. [Benchmark decoder](../../app/scripts/bench.ts) · [Production matcher](../../app/src/lib/dress/item-palette.ts) · [Ramp derivation](../../app/src/lib/dress/palette.ts)

- **Production** uses the current maximum 12 levels in each RGB channel, with at least a three-level Euclidean distance gap to the second-nearest standing color. The closest candidate must belong to an existing dye ramp. Unowned colors still compete.
- **Wider** keeps the same ownership and distance-gap rules but permits 24 levels per channel.
- **Owned-only** compares only to owned standing colors, permits 24 levels per channel, and accepts the nearest without an ambiguity gap. All variants leave already exact standing or owned colors alone.
- **Edge veto** starts with the production mapping, then cancels each changed source color implicated in any previously distinct, opaque four-neighbor edge that would become a single RGB. The veto applies throughout the clip to avoid choosing different colors for the same RGB from frame to frame.
- **Edge graph** also starts with the production mapping. Build an undirected graph of distinct RGB pairs that touch in the supplied frames. Consider each proposed color replacement by smallest Euclidean change, breaking ties by the number of affected pixels and then RGB. Apply the proposal only if no neighbor has its target RGB after previous decisions. This retains one of two otherwise-conflicting replacements when possible. It measures and preserves RGB edges, without labeling their artistic purpose.

No policy changed alpha. Edge counts include only neighboring opaque pixels that differed in both the original and perturbed artwork, and each horizontal or vertical edge is counted once. Leaving the perturbed RGB alone restored zero exact pixels and removed zero additional edges. Strong perturbations make a wrong standing color look like a close match. At 20 levels the current rule wrote more wrong owned shades than exact restorations. An automatically rewritten pixel is therefore an unreliable proxy for a corrected pixel.

All 520 production merges in the live replay occur in north Walk. That view illustrates the tradeoff. Production binds 1,394 pixels, strict veto binds 57, and the graph binds 691 with no measured edge loss. Larger automatic tolerances mostly change the kinds of errors. The graph and veto give different conservative candidates for native-scale artist review, with a different cost in dye coverage.

Three synthetic controls expose limits even when the color difference looks small. With unowned `#202020` and owned `#30202a` standing colors, the production matcher abstains on `#252026` while owned-only assigns it to `#30202a`. With metal `#535f69` and leather `#696053`, production abstains on the near tie `#5e605e` while owned-only assigns it to leather. Both an intentionally new one-pixel emblem and nearby cloth can arrive as the same `#3e263a`. Every matcher in the comparison turns both into the approved cloth shade `#3c2338`. Edge veto and the graph cannot separate two uses of the same incoming RGB. An exact RGB that belongs to the wrong material also bypasses every matcher. A stronger automatic route therefore needs independent pixel ownership evidence, such as approved motif masks or reliable correspondence, before assigning ambiguous edges, details, and materials. Keep edge-preserving alternatives as reviewed comparisons until that evidence is qualified.
