## All-frame observations

- Case 01 A has large key-colored streaks in the right reference region during early frames and evolves from a closer vest into a wider, less structured endpoint. B is comparatively restrained. These transient artifacts matter to process reliability even when only an endpoint is retained.
- Both case 02 sequences alter the detached reference before attaching it. The final forward object/trim near the hand remains difficult to classify. A key-colored body can still be approximately aligned while a generated hand or outline fragment is misclassified as equipment.
- Both case 03 sequences eventually dress the body. B has a bright key-colored replacement of the detached garment in early frames before it returns and attaches. A's tunic grows wider toward the endpoint.
- Case 04 A temporarily creates a body/arm-colored appendage in the detached reference region. B has an early nearly white whole-body flash. Both later produce recognizable green clothing, but the newly invented golden structures remain.

No obvious gross final translation was established visually in the closer candidates, but visual inspection cannot certify exact anatomical correspondence. Frozen-body recomposition is useful for judging the final appearance and can also hide mistakes in the raw body. Inspect raw deltas, independently labeled gear and protected pixels separately. The last-frame sensitivity does not visibly rescue the conspicuous motif/design failures. No generation default or cloth-physics benefit is accepted by this visual review alone.
