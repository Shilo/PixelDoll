# Free animation and rigging sources for PixelDoll

Research date: October 27, 2026. This companion report covers external sources; the coordinating investigation owns local failure diagnosis and PixelLab tests. This is a broad, prioritized survey, not an exhaustive inventory of every animation project. No packages were installed, no new animation archives were imported, no paid calls were made, and no application behavior changed for this report. Asset counts below are publisher claims unless stated otherwise. Exact downloadable archive contents and browser integration were not tested here.

## Findings that change the recommendation

1. **Keep Quaternius as a motion source, but audit the exact edition and version already baked.** Its official UAL1 changelog records fixes for elbow twist, export frame rate, directional step phase, and root motion. Those are directly relevant failure modes, but their existence does not establish that an old archive caused PixelDoll's failures. A good 3D animation remains only an input to a separate 2D retargeting problem. [UAL1 release notes and editions](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library)
2. **Use current KayKit as an independent second source.** The publisher offers all animation clips free under CC0, with separate medium and large rigs. This is a better diagnostic comparison than collecting many indistinguishable mocap packs. [KayKit Character Animations](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/kaykit-character-animations)
3. **The most relevant permissive 3D-to-2D research implementation is Meta's Animated Drawings.** Its MIT code implements motion transfer to drawn characters, including configurable projection groups and depth handling. It is a research reference, not evidence of professional pixel-art output. Its Python pipeline is unsuitable as an unchanged dependency of this client-only app. [Repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/AnimatedDrawings), [retargeter implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/AnimatedDrawings/blob/main/animated_drawings/model/retargeter.py)
4. **A small original pose solver plus Three.js for source sampling is the strongest fit.** A full 2D animation runtime does not improve an AI renderer's pose adherence. It helps only if PixelDoll adopts a separate cutout or mesh-rendering output path.
5. **No surveyed tool establishes a guarantee for every humanoid shape, size, sprite, and direction.** Qualification needs defined proportion families and per-direction review. “Works on any humanoid” is an unverified product promise.

### Useful alternatives outside the requested MIT/CC0 shortlist

- [Babylon.js retargeting](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/animation/animationRetargeting) is browser-capable and explicitly handles rest-pose compensation, mapping, and root/ground adjustments. Its [engine license is Apache-2.0](https://github.com/BabylonJS/Babylon.js/blob/master/license.md), not MIT. This is an excellent reference for tests even if not adopted. Source and target reference poses must be correct; frame zero is not necessarily a rest pose.
- [closed-chain-ik-js](https://github.com/gkjohnson/closed-chain-ik-js) uses Apache-2.0, not MIT. Its generalized damped-least-squares system is excessive for two elbows and two knees. Consider only if real multi-contact requirements exceed simpler solvers.
- [Blender](https://www.blender.org/about/license/) is GPL software, not MIT. The official license page distinguishes artwork/output from application code. Use it offline to curate CC0 motion, correct contacts, and bake sprites/pose data without making it part of PixelDoll's browser runtime. Source asset licenses continue to apply to output.
- [Spine runtimes](https://esotericsoftware.com/spine-runtimes-license) have their own editor-related license conditions. Public source is not MIT permission. Keep them outside this free permissive dependency shortlist.
- [Spriter.ts](https://github.com/flyover/spriter.ts) was surveyed as a TypeScript SCML/SCON reader. No license was established from the inspected repository page and attempted license URL. Do not assume MIT or copy it pending an explicit license review.

## What the research means for the failure

The following is engineering inference, not a new measured PixelDoll result.

A correct mocap trajectory is a motion on a specific 3D body. Directly projecting its joint positions onto a differently proportioned sprite preserves neither target bone lengths nor support contacts. Conversely, copying only screen-space angle changes while keeping every visible limb at full rest length can turn depth-directed motion into a large lateral swing. When a 3D bone points toward the camera, its projected vector approaches zero; an angle inferred from that vector becomes unstable. Neither a different prompt nor another motion library removes that geometric ambiguity.

The [Animated Drawings paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12741) studies a twisted-perspective retargeting method rather than assuming one camera projection is universally readable for drawn figures. Its [implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/AnimatedDrawings/blob/main/animated_drawings/model/retargeter.py) assigns projection planes and depth drivers to groups. This supports testing projection-aware motion adaptation, but does not prove that its drawing deformation style meets PixelDoll's pixel-art quality bar.

The alternatives address different parts of the problem:

| Approach | Motion consistency | New visible surfaces and turns | Pixel identity and equipment alignment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Curated poses sent to PixelLab skeleton-v3 | Deterministic requested poses; rendered adherence must be measured | AI can redraw them, with variable quality | Whole-body redrawing can still change details or alignment |
| Native 2D cutout or mesh rig | Deterministic after rig/animation authoring | Requires prepared directional parts or replacement drawings | Predictable transforms, but rotated/scaled pixels and seams need cleanup |
| Retargeted 3D proxy rendered into sprites | Deterministic motion and camera | Yes, if a suitable 3D character and equipment exist | Consistent render layers; different product/art workflow from animating one supplied sprite |
| Hand-authored raster pose families | Most art-directed control | Each required pose/view is authored | Strongest pixel control, highest labor, limited automatic coverage |

## Recommended proof pipeline

1. **Define the qualification envelope.** Start with one ordinary humanoid, one chibi, and one tall/slender humanoid at representative native resolutions, each in south and east. Later qualify all supported directions and sizes. Missing/ambiguous anatomy should require joint correction rather than an “any sprite” claim.
2. **Lock provenance and timing.** Choose one Quaternius clip and an equivalent current KayKit clip. Record archive hash, version, license, skeleton mapping, rest transforms, units, axes, handedness, source rate, in-place/root-motion choice, and original duration. Verify that the imported source looks good before retargeting. Preserve original sample times; authored anticipation/contact/recovery timing is part of the animation.
3. **Calibrate the sprite separately from the source rest pose.** Store anatomical left/right, native-pixel coordinates, segment proportions, planted-foot plane, facing/camera, and hand identity. Visually approve the reference-joint overlay. Do not use frame zero of an action as an assumed bind pose.
4. **Retarget with explicit constraints.** Prefer a target-proportioned 3D proxy plus FK and contact IK when geometry can be fitted credibly. For a lighter 2D approach, preserve drawn proportions while allowing bounded foreshortening and stable bend direction; handle near-zero projected bones explicitly. Treat hips, torso, head, and limbs separately. Any fixed-facing adaptation should be clip-specific and reviewed, because removing all twist can also damage natural movement.
5. **Represent intent, not just points.** Each curated preset needs support-foot intervals, ground contacts, strike/impact time, grip hand, loop boundaries, allowed root motion, occlusion ordering, and direction-specific pose corrections. IK should enforce those authored contacts; it cannot invent their meaning.
6. **Approve a free preview first.** Show 3D source, retargeted stick figure, optional rough sprite puppet, and per-frame joint overlay together at native scale and slowed speed. Check foot planting, knee/elbow stability, silhouette, torso/head behavior, bounds, timing, and loops. A puppet preview predicts motion readability, not AI output or newly visible pixels.
7. **Run controlled paired PixelLab tests.** Hold character, clip, times, seed, wording, size, and direction fixed while changing one cause: source/calibration, retarget method, timing, projection, or prompt. Include a hand-polished skeleton control and repeat selected conditions because one seed can flatter a method. Avoid thousands of uncontrolled generations.
8. **Freeze only owner-approved presets.** Store exact accepted poses, durations, provenance, corrected reference joints, outputs, and known failures. Reuse pose templates by proportion family with calibration and a review gate. A named preset is a curated motion contract, not a raw mocap clip.

The minimum useful dependency set remains small: existing image/queue infrastructure, original pose math, and optional Three.js in bake tooling. Add glTF Transform only for a demonstrated asset-processing need. Keep Blender or Godot as optional offline authoring companions. A switch to mesh rigs or rendered 3D characters would be a separate product decision, not an incidental repair.

## Research limits and validation still needed

- No claim is made that any shortlisted asset or solver already improves PixelDoll's output. This report supplies candidates and discriminating tests.
- The exact Standard-pack clip inventory, bundled notices, animation curves, and root-motion conventions must be checked in the downloaded archive used for implementation. Publisher marketing totals are not free-edition counts.
- License labels here describe inspected first-party statements, not every example file, transitive dependency, community upload, or future release. Retain notices for copied MIT code; retain CC0 provenance even though attribution is optional.
- CMU FAQ access failed; detailed terms remain unverified. The YouTube video could not be watched. No conclusions rely on those unavailable contents.
- Quality metrics need owner-approved visual anchors. A low joint error does not measure appeal, naturalness, stable pixels, or correct contact. A renderer can follow an uncanny input accurately.
- Full eight-direction, all-body-type consistency requires testing across that matrix. A successful south/east proof is evidence for a next step, not proof of universal support.
