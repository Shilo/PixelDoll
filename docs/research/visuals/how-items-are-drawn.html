How Items Are Drawn

The same bare character and the same red cloak, made two ways. Every picture below is a real stored frame from our PixelLab tests, run through the app's own code. Drag the slider or press Play to step through the animation; every step updates together.

Does PixelLab redraw the item every frame?

Yes, in both ways. Watch step 4 in each column: the cloak is a new PixelLab drawing in every frame. Our code never moves or rotates the cloak.

So what is the difference?

Which body PixelLab draws on. Today it draws a brand-new body plus cloak and we keep only the cloak. The other way, it paints the cloak onto the exact frames you see.

What is the "1 px shift"?

Step 6 on the left. If sliding PixelLab's whole new drawing by one pixel lines it up better with the real body, the app slides it. That is all. It never rotates and never moves more than 1 px.

Local code · chromaLayer(), then postProcess() cleanup

Every green pixel is removed, which leaves only the cloak. Shown here before the app's cleanup pass. In frame 6 PixelLab also drew a sword nobody asked for; stray props like that are what the cleanup and quality checks are there to catch. Weapons get one more rule: if the weapon disappeared on a nearly still frame, the standing pose's weapon is pasted at the hand and the frame is flagged for review.

Where the gens go

One worn item (the cloak) for idle and walk, on an 80 px character with 3 drawn directions (south, east, north; west is a mirror). Each block is one PixelLab call, sized by its price. The character's own animation is already paid for and is the same for both.

Dress10 S3E3N3
  • edit-animation-v2 × 1: dress the 3 standing views in one call = 10
  • animate-with-skeleton-v3 × 3: one per direction, idle 4 + walk 8 frames together, 3 gens each = 9
Dress the real frames70 gens · 7 calls
Dress10 Idle S10Idle E10Idle N10 Walk S10Walk E10Walk N10
  • edit-animation-v2 × 1: dress the 3 standing views = 10
  • edit-animation-v2 × 6: one per move per direction (reference + up to 8 frames each), 10 gens each = 60

Why the gap: the skeleton tool charges 3 gens for a call of up to 12 frames, so one call covers a whole direction. The edit tool charges 10 gens per call and fits at most 8 frames plus the reference at 80 px, so every move in every direction is its own 10-gen call. With the default 5 drawn directions it is 25 gens (1 dress + 5 pose calls) against 110 (1 dress + 10 edit calls). Above 80 px the edit tool fits only 3 frames per call, so the gap grows further.

Why items look stiff in the app today: PixelLab does redraw them every frame, but it redraws them in the poses we give it. The app's own idle moves the upper body only 0–1 px and our authored moves move only the arms, so the item barely moves. Hair has no joints, so it moves with the head as one piece. The fast slash on the left shows that the same route moves the cloak a lot when the body moves a lot.
