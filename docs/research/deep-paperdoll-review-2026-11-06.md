# PixelDoll generation and output quality review

November 6, 2026. The review follows the [October 27 method](paperdoll-repos-review-2026-10-27.md) and rechecks the [earlier tool review](tool-review-2026-11-06.md) and its four spikes. The scope runs from standing design and prompt construction through pose conditioning, provider output, extraction, equipment review, dye, composition, and export. Provider statements, offline results, live observations, and hypotheses are recorded separately.

## Changes qualified in the application

1. **Register against visible mannequin evidence.** Off-key opaque pixels may be equipment, so their silhouette can no longer pull a keyed frame to a different position. A synthetic case proved that whole-alpha IoU preferred the wrong shift. A separate test showed why registration must abstain when a nearly covered mannequin has only sparse key evidence. The protected-grid case still rejects a genuine one-pixel offset and never translates across the submitted stencil. The 20-case frozen benchmark kept all expected verdicts and pixel-label totals. In the registration replay, one retained cloak frame moved up one pixel, where visible-witness IoU improved from 0.808 to 0.829. Its clip still failed G3 as expected.
2. **Evaluate weapon shape on the real playback sequence.** Hard continuity checks follow authored adjacent cells. A repeated cell contributes no transition and a missing candidate does not create a false jump. A loop-only shape or angle jump appears in equipment review without becoming a hard interior failure. This leaves export playback preferences independent of paid quality gates. The tests include one-shot rotation, reordered and repeated cells, a missing frame, a loop-only jump, and a real interior discontinuity.
3. **Use the fitted pose quote for the actual job reservation.** A shared fit for 9–12 frames previously reserved four generations after quoting three and could reject its own plan before submitting. The request builder now uses the same estimator as the quote. The fix does not reinterpret ambiguous provider pricing.
4. **Save completed equipment without overwriting outfit edits.** Publication still guards rig and item artwork in the atomic update. It now preserves the current material membership and hide settings, which outfit autosave can change independently. Price review also ignores these display settings. If an anchored item-only move hits the save conflict, **Use saved frames** reconstructs the exact paid request identities and reuses only completed matches. This path performs no enqueue or provider request. New anchored jobs carry the move’s activity scope so cleanup retains their receipts while the flow needs them. The exact field that caused the original Walk save conflict was not captured. The metadata race and the true-artwork conflict both have regression coverage. The compact repair reproduced a preflight rejection on a fresh import, and the same quote proceeded after the signature fix.
5. **Preserve approved dyes across anchored moves.** Compare each extracted frame to its own facing’s approved standing layer before normalization. One bad frame can now trigger palette review even if the others are close. A second local pass maps a generated shade only when its closest approved shade belongs to an existing dye ramp, it differs by at most 12 in any channel, and the next candidate is at least three RGB-distance units farther. All standing colors compete, including unowned outlines. Colors already present in the approved palette or an owned ramp are preserved. Alpha and position stay exact. The local correction leaves paid provenance and the raw result bytes in the normal job cache. A clean standing item that has never been worn derives its dye ramps from the approved art and publishes those ramps atomically with the new move. Dense and absent standing palettes abstain. The corrected item layer uses the existing exact dye and export contract.
6. **Validate described body motion before it can spend.** An imported move name could be routed to PixMiniMax without a description and quoted without its motion and first joint-check costs. An incompatible count could also reach the provider before raw Skeleton v3’s limit stopped the render. Studio now keeps the selected replacement name, requires a description, and limits the current described pipeline to counts supported by both services. It checks actual returned cardinality before paying for joint checks on that view. [PixelLab REST schema](https://api.pixellab.ai/v2/openapi.json)
7. **Show the cut body in equipment diagnostics.** The M3 workbench, benchmark contact sheets and split verification now use the same body-cut operation as Studio. A frozen greathelm test compares every RGBA byte of the diagnostic composite against the expected equipment pixels at recorded cuts and the original composite everywhere else.
8. **Export the complete body-cut combination.** Simultaneous hood and cape cuts now select one deterministic body variant containing both masks in Godot, Phaser, Unity, GDevelop and Aseprite. Material ownership stays attached to the original body. Mirrored engine-sheet imports materialize cut coordinates before removing mirror metadata, and padded or cropped body layers map rig indices through their pivot offset. The full inventory is qualified up to 256 generated body variants and refuses larger expansions before artwork allocation. [Ownership follow-up and native controls](deep-paperdoll-ownership-followup-2026-11-06.md)
9. **Keep temporal cleanup within its move.** An Idle or Run frame can no longer vote to preserve a transient Walk component. Exact RGBA controls preserve genuine same-clip extensions and isolate other directions and partial inputs. All 20 frozen benchmark cases retain their expected verdicts. Same-clip batch partition differences remain an explicit limit. [Clip-local cleanup follow-up](deep-paperdoll-geometry-2026-11-06.md#clip-local-cleanup-follow-up)
10. **Review each posed frame's palette.** Skeleton posing and grid repair now use the shared direction gate to compare every nonempty posed frame against its own standing design. One recolored frame cannot hide behind seven matching ones. Overall and per-clip saved palette maxima include that standing comparison. This extends the earlier anchored-edit check, changes no artwork and remains a warning for review. [Matched trial audit and controls](deep-paperdoll-live-trials-2026-11-06.md#decisions-and-remaining-work)

## Live browser evidence

The test used a local copy of the public Adventurer demo and an editable import assembled from the retained 80 px benchmark. Nine requests completed for $0.72 and zero subscription generations. The original standing doublet purchase was a prior session. This round generated Walk for three authored directions and Cross Punch for south. Walk initially hit the publication guard after all three calls completed. The subsequent reload showed Walk published from the same paid jobs, with no additional Walk purchase. Cross Punch published in a fresh run. Cache-only recovery has separate simulated conflict and missing-cache coverage.

The first post-run PNG + JSON archive contained 491 entries. Walk had eight frames in each of four directions, with an exact horizontal west mirror. Cross Punch had six south frames. The doublet’s saved checks reported 0.979 minimum key retention across standing and moves, zero key silhouette expansion, zero framing pixels and zero rerolls. Every tested opaque animated pixel differed from the same-facing standing palette despite a passing QA summary. The mean nearest-color distances by frame were 4.7–9.7, which explains why the old metric could pass the output while exact palette swapping missed it.

Applying the new production correction to the very same exported original sprites gives this controlled comparison. Mirrored west is omitted from the total.

| Original output | Pixels restored to an owned standing shade | Opaque pixels | Largest channel change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Walk south | 1,301 | 2,116 | 7 |
| Walk east | 666 | 1,084 | 11 |
| Walk north | 1,394 | 2,103 | 12 |
| Cross Punch south | 469 | 1,176 | 5 |
| Total | 3,830 | 6,479 | 12 |

No alpha bytes changed. The rule abstains on distant clasp colors and ambiguous dark shades. It also cannot recover a clasp or hem the provider did not draw. This replay isolates the improvement from variation across paid generations.

A second challenge measured 520 small color boundaries merged by this correction in the north Walk. A boundary-preserving candidate bound 3,127 pixels across the same clips with zero measured edge merges. Synthetic controls showed why even that candidate cannot distinguish an emblem from cloth when they share the same incoming RGB. The expanded dye study records where the current default helps, where it can alter detail, and why greater tolerances need pixel ownership evidence. [Dye policy challenge](deep-paperdoll-vision-2026-11-06.md#how-far-can-automatic-dye-matching-go)

**Second live design.** A short indigo cape used one standing and three Walk edit requests, costing $0.32. Its three authored Walk views passed, with zero soft-alpha pixels and maximum per-view palette distances of 8.1, 3.5 and 4.8. The same-facing standing palette accounted for 711/1,429, 102/1,233 and 2,866/2,902 opaque pixels in south, east and north. Side-view and outline differences still limit exact dye coverage. The 570-entry archive retained separate front and back layers and reported the missing Cross Punch coverage.

**Compact frozen repair.** The `bodyB` and `b-tunic` fixtures were assembled into an 80 px editable import with the fourth east Walk layer deliberately removed. Studio showed 10 estimated generations and submitted exactly one `inpaint-v3` call with a 240 × 240 mask and image, eight target cells and one protected reference. PixelLab reported $0.08. The result passed and restored 181 opaque pixels in the missing frame. All eight repaired frames had binary alpha. Only the east equipment layer hashes changed. The standing references and all body hashes matched the pre-repair body after reproducing `splitBase`, the app’s import-time color normalization. Comparing new masks with the original `runCase` result gives IoU 0.649–0.766 across eight cells. The test item’s prompt incorrectly described the green reference as red, and the result still used predominantly green artwork. This run verifies the smaller request geometry and successful frame recovery. A matched 320 × 240 control with the correct description is necessary to compare price or design fidelity across layouts.

## Experiments for provider-side changes

Use pairs from the same frozen bitmap, artwork revision, requested pose and seed. Log provider version when exposed, request geometry, complete receipts, actual cash and generation charges, wall time, protected-pixel violations, candidate retention, missing motifs and blinded visual judgment at native scale. Include intentionally correct occlusion as a control. A new request route needs repeatable improvement on multiple designs and both regular and high-occlusion motions before promotion.

| Priority | Comparison and decision rule |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Same-character reference transfer.** Compare the current editable standing anchor to `transfer-outfit-v2` using the same approved reference, 8 frozen targets and facing instructions. The transfer accepts a distinct reference and returns targets. Judge sleeve coverage, motif survival, recolorability and provider cost. Handle the two-target minimum and authored transparency explicitly. [PixelLab Transfer Outfit](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/transfer-outfit-pro) |
| 2 | **Canonical skeleton source.** Compare the raw dressed composite and the derived frozen keyed body plus the approved gear and cuts. Send the same frozen target joints. Start with opaque rigid headwear, then include cape, footwear and held-out action views. Keep standing layer hashes and paid raw artifacts as independent facts. [PixelLab REST schema](https://api.pixellab.ai/v2/openapi.json) |
| 3 | **Shared chroma ramp.** Compare current per-cell and continuous body-stable luminance with matching standing and move keys. Include imported bodies, atypically dark and light designs and prompts that collide with keys. Assess outfit extraction and material fidelity together. Cache identity must include the changed request bitmap and the standing key policy. |
| 4 | **Conservative spatial repair.** In 168 held-out frames, two-pixel neighboring support retained all greathelm item pixels but missed 35 body-cut pixels. Including the neighboring cuts recovered all 329 cuts and sharply reduced editable background. Three-pixel fixed neighbors covered the tested tunic, while even four pixels missed over 20% of one valid cape frame. Test rigid headwear first, with known visibility and cut support. Keep the broad band for loose garments and uncertain cases. [Frozen stencil experiment](deep-paperdoll-conditioning-2026-11-06.md#leave-one-frame-out-repair-stencils-on-frozen-renders) · [PixelLab Inpaint v3](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/inpaint-v3) |
| 5 | **Visibility-aware equipment.** Qualify wrist-relative blade polarity and per-pixel depth on accepted/rejected held, fringe, cape and shield cases. Use sparse artist or trusted geometry ownership for known protected pixels and abstain on ambiguous overlaps. Export must reproduce preview under combinations and mirrors. Evaluate shape and attribution per playback transition across batch boundaries. |
| 6 | **Per-frame body review.** Across 136 retained frames in 17 clips, one passing walk averaged 1.40 px arm disagreement while one estimated wrist differed from the requested joint by 7.19 px. Another passing clip averaged 99.51% palette fit but had a 96.59% frame. All checked hand, ankle and head witnesses landed on opaque pixels across the 88 measured walk frames. These are review candidates. Pair them with visible-side contact and use local confidence before changing hard body gates. [Footskate Cleanup](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/graphics/Gallery/kovar.vol/Cleanup/cleanup.pdf) · [Contact-Aware Retargeting](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Villegas_Contact-Aware_Retargeting_of_Skinned_Motion_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf) |

The [completed matched campaign](deep-paperdoll-live-trials-2026-11-06.md) tests priorities 1, 2 and 3 on Walk, two body types, five designs, two views and two seeds. Its mixed results do not qualify a generation default change. A [prepared target-palette pilot](deep-paperdoll-target-palette-2026-11-06.md) changes only the palette field against eight completed controls. High-occlusion actions, weapons and reference-mask variants remain untested in that matrix. The authenticated Civitai graph is still unavailable, despite verified public metadata. These are explicit remaining experiments; this review makes no exhaustive internet or model-output coverage claim.
