# CHALLENGE: adversarial review of the pipeline spike

2026-11-24. Review sub-agent. Read-only: no paid calls, no tests, no app edits. Evidence checked against `app/src/lib/items/items.ts`, `app/src/lib/rig/clips.ts`, `docs/known-issues.md`, `docs/research/pixellab/pixellab-openapi-v2.json`, `docs/research/2026-11-23-item-move-route-cost.md` (x31 usage records), and R1-R5. Findings are ordered by how much they move the recommendation, not by how easy they are to fix.

## Blockers

**B1. The headline cost table is wrong in both columns, and the wrong numbers carry the argument.** README "What was verified" says skeleton is 25 gen for a new item's first move and 15 per later move, and the sheet is 35 and 25. Code gives one pose call per clip-direction at 3 gen for 6 frames (`poseCallGens`, `clips.ts:233`) and one dress call at 10 gen at 92 px (`editGens`, `items.ts:757-760`), so a 3-view first move is 10 + 3x3 = **19**, and a later move is **9**. The sheet at 3 views x 6 frames is 392x270, mean 331, which the app prices **15** (`meanSideGens`, `items.ts:229-232`) and which x31 billed 15 (`392x270 = 15 (x3)`, `2026-11-23-item-move-route-cost.md:39`); the README's own R5 table says the same (R5:36-38). So the real pair is 25 vs 19 and 15 vs 9, not 35 vs 25 and 25 vs 15. R5 also derived 19, and the README's 25/15 looks like a 5-view skeleton row pasted onto a 3-view label. The inpaint row is wrong the same way: `gridEstimate(92, 6, 1, 4)` is 368x184, mean 276, so 15 per call and **45** for 3 calls, not 75. Fix: rebuild the table from `planItem` + `poseGensFor` + `meanSideGens` and the x31 usage records, pin one view count (3 requested views for the app's default mirrored 4-direction rig), and recompute every downstream "accepted asset" figure. The qualitative conclusion survives (a sheet costs about 6 gen more per attempt on both moves), but the README currently claims it is 10 gen more, and the 50-70 / 28-39 yield numbers are derived from 5-view prices.

**B2. The flagship layout cannot be produced by the app.** The README's core arithmetic ("5 views x 8 frames", "45 cells in 504x450", the 5-view columns of the size table, P1.1) is blocked by `canSheet`: every rig direction must be cardinal or a mirror, and every requested direction must be cardinal (`items.ts:892-898`). `dirs` can be at most the four cardinals, so **4 rows is the ceiling**; an 8-direction rig with diagonals fails the gate outright. Yet the table's 5-view column is what produces the 40-55% per-frame saving and the "80 px knee: 11 frames x 5 views". At 4 views the same 45-cell sheet is 10 frames (92 px) and 14 frames (80 px), which is better coverage than stated, and the 80 px "11 frames" figure is an artefact of an impossible view count. Fix: mark every 5-view row "raw API probe, needs a `canSheet` relaxation", or delete it; restate coverage at 4 views; add "diagonals and 8-direction rigs are not sheet-eligible today" to Limits, and drop P1.1's 5-view design (it is currently 150 gen for a shape the app cannot ship).

**B3. Sheet eligibility is much narrower than the recommendation admits.** `canSheet` requires 80-128 px cells, no held slot, a saved standing design in every requested view, `canAnchor` (route `ea2-anchor`/`skeleton-pose`, layers already on the rig cell), and cardinal-or-mirrored directions. So the sheet is not available at 64 px, not at 160 px, not for padded layers, not for any item whose layer cell differs from the rig cell, and not before the standing dress has been paid for. The README lists only "worn, non-held, 4-direction, 64 px has swaps". Fix: replace that line with the exact predicate, and say which catalog share it actually covers (the README already assumes a per-slot policy is unmeasured; this makes the default claim narrower, not wider).

**B4. The default re-proposes an idea the decision record rejected, with no new evidence from this spike.** R4 lists "Painting gear directly on the real frames as the default" (about 110 gen per worn item over 5 views vs about 25; transfer failed 2 of 16 clips at the gates while posing failed 0 of 12) and "Code-placed / tracked rigid head layers" (re-posed helmets already match the painted-on control) as rejected (`R4:31,33`). The README recommends both (A and B). This spike's own 36 gen tested the multi-image cap and 2x-downscale, not sheet quality, so the new evidence is prior x31 data that had no skeleton arm. Worse, the stated motivation is contradicted by the accepted record: delivered posed gear already fits the frozen base at 0.2-1.4 px/frame with a **0 px grip gap on all 129 stored held frames** (`R4:20-21`), while the "3 of 4 items fail the body-kept gate" is a raw-output G2 failure (x31:119), not a defect in the worn layer. Fix: demote A to "pilot, pending a paired delivered-layer comparison"; cite R4 directly where the recommendation overrides it; and stop using G2 as a product-quality claim.

**B5. Held/rigid warp (B) hides the mechanisms that would decide it.** Rotating a layer about the wrist changes which body pixels it covers in every frame, so the over-body / behind-body split and the hand-over-grip treatment must be recomputed per frame. They are currently per-item, and exported body cuts are a per-item combination capped at 256 variants (`docs/known-issues.md:114-117,157`); a rotation can push a wardrobe past that cap before a sheet is generated. "Occlusion stays with the existing cuts and z-order" is false. The test is also circular: grip <=2 px is satisfied by construction, and "0 px on 129 frames" means B has no measured accuracy gain over today. Fix: make the offline prototype output the per-frame body-mask/combination count and require no increase over the current count; judge on rim/edge quality and blind grip/occlusion on stored clips, not grip distance.

**B6. The adoption gate cannot be evaluated as written.** Direction A's decision rule needs "a blind preference over the skeleton control in >=6 of 8 pairs", but Phase 1 generates no skeleton arm, generation is not bit-repeatable (`docs/known-issues.md:106-107`), and P1.1 provides 6 units, not 8 pairs. A seed is therefore not a pairing key, and the only pairing that exists is by item. Fix: budget the control (3 items x 4 directions x a 6-frame pose is 3 items x 12 gen = 36, plus 30 dress = 66, or fewer units with fewer views), state pairing is by item until reproducibility is measured, and align the decision rule's N with the actual units.

## Major

**M1. Accepted-asset economics is unmeasured and asymmetric.** R5 itself says no acceptance rate exists (R5:64) and that the app's retry model is a scenario, yet the README prices 50% skeleton vs 90% sheet and concludes the recommendation "rests on that accepted-asset economics". The retry units differ by an order of magnitude: a pose failure is 3 gen and per clip-direction; a sheet failure is 25 for the whole sheet or 10 per view, and per-view salvage is a separate paid result (so "without changing passing views" is true by construction, not a guarantee of design consistency). Fix: delete the 50-70 / 28-39 sentence, or label it "illustrative with invented p"; measure `sum(gens)/accepted` and the retry unit per route.

**M2. Review cost is excluded but is the binding constraint.** `dirGate` does not run on edit cells and the coverage-signature prototype false-alarmed on 9-26% of real frames (x31:130), so the sheet route needs per-cell human review: 3 views x 10 frames = 30 cells per item-move at 92 px, versus 3 dressed views for skeleton. The README says review "is the real cost" and then omits it from every comparison, including the phase plan, which budgets no review time and no rater. Fix: log reviewed cells and minutes per unit, include them in the accepted-asset comparison, and make Phase 3 (a gate) a precondition for adopting A rather than a parallel phase.

**M3. Phase 3 tests the failure mode the gate is admitted not to catch.** The corpus is whole-row swaps injected into passing sheets; the README's own limit is "catches whole-row replacement, not single-frame swaps", while the only observed real failures are single-frame front/back swaps (4/216 at 64 px) and 3 of 79 wrong dressed views that passed every check (`docs/known-issues.md:223-235`). 180 gen therefore buys a gate that still cannot authorize auto-save, so A keeps human review and loses the cost case. Fix: include the stored 64 px swap frames and single-frame injections; report precision/recall on that corpus; only then state whether review can be dropped.

**M4. The size/coverage table ignores actual cell sizing and the already-proven 15-gen tier.** `itemSheetWindows` uses one cell size for every view, from the widest content over the move's body frames and the reference plus 8 px, capped at the frame (`items.ts:956-993`). A cape, hat or back item makes the cell equal the frame and collapses cells per sheet (at 92 px: 5 columns x 4 rows = 20 cells, not 45), so P1.2's capes are the worst case for the table, not a footnote. Separately, x31's 6x4 layout of 56x84 cells (336x336) already passed 8/8 for **15 gen** (x31:58), which is 24 cells; the real per-cell gain from 25/45 is about 22%, not the claimed 40-55%, and 60 cells again needs the 5-view row that `canSheet` rejects. Fix: recompute per measured item with `itemSheetWindows`, add the 15-gen 24-cell row, delete the 40-55% claim.

**M5. Registration and extraction assumptions are under-specified.** `sheetOffset` finds one whole-sheet +/-5 px offset, but rows are returned separately and differ by 2-20% of shape, so a global offset cannot fix per-row drift. `ITEM_SHEET.margin` is 4 px, less than the 5 px reach, so the chosen offset can cut a cell into a neighbour; `cropEdgeContact` already exists to catch exactly that and is not in the plan. Fix: use `cropEdgeContact` (and a per-row offset if needed) as an extraction gate; set margin >= reach or reach <= margin; test drift per row.

**M6. Phase budgets do not fund the plan.** Phases 1-3 are 720, not 760; 720 + 200 reserve = 920 of 964, leaving 44 unallocated. P2.3 is under-costed: `transfer-outfit-v2` caps at 3 frames above 80 px, so 6 frames is 2 calls per view, and 2 items x 3 views x 2 = 12 calls, about 120 gen, not 40-60 (at 40 it can only test one view per item, which cannot answer grip across views). The skeleton control arm from B6 is unbudgeted. Fix: restate 720 + 244 free, raise P2.3 to 120 and name its views, and pay for the control by cutting Phase 3 or P2.2.

**M7. Mirrored views are hidden.** On the app's default 4-direction rig the sheet's west row uses the **mirrored east dress** as its reference, so the sheet cannot unflip a one-sided item; adopting "every requested view in one sheet" either keeps the flip or changes west art relative to the mirrored pipeline, and `docs/known-issues.md:108-113` records the handedness defect. Fix: state the west-row policy and add one one-sided item (eyepatch, single pauldron, quiver) to P1.1.

**M8. Two hidden risks in direction C and in wall clock.** `ITEM_SHEET.maxCell` is 128, so the 160 px hero sheet in C is not a sheet the app can request (and at 160 the layer plus any pad saturates the 256 px export cap, per R2:32). A 25-gen 512 px sheet is one long job, while `itemGenerationMinutes` counts calls, so C and A look faster than they are versus 3-gen pose calls. Fix: cap C at 128 or state the export change; record wall clock per call in the ledger.

## Minor

**m1. P2.4 cannot answer its own question.** "Shape IoU >=0.85 vs the accepted frame" compares a deterministic transform with a layer that itself drifted, and the stored east axe is a single experimental import (`docs/known-issues.md:238`); agreement with a drifting result is not correctness. Fix: compare against the body frame's silhouette and attachment, and accept on a blind ranking plus the body-variant count.

**m2. The target_palette challenge misstates R1.** R1 already wrote "not typed on `EditImagesV2`" (R1:67); my schema check confirms `EditImagesV2Request` has only `method, edit_images, image_size, description, reference_image, seed, no_background`, so the README's conclusion is right but the accusation is not. The rejection also does not transfer to `transfer-outfit-v2`, which does have `target_palette` (schema checked) and is P2.3's held route. Fix: correct the wording and either test the field on transfer-outfit or say why not.

**m3. The 35-gen sheet row is inconsistent.** It includes the 10-gen dress; the 25-gen 5-view row does not, and at 92 px five views need two dress calls (20 gen), so both rows understate a new item's first move. Fix: one "new item" convention column.

## Verdict

The sheet-first default is **not justified on the current evidence**. The arithmetic in the README's central table is wrong (B1), the headline layout is not reachable through the app's own eligibility gate (B2/B3), the comparison lacks the skeleton arm the decision rule requires (B6), and the recommendation re-proposes a default the decision record rejected while the accepted fit data (0.2-1.4 px misfit, 0 px grip gap) undercuts the body-drift motive (B4/B5). What survives is a narrower, real finding: a single-image all-view sheet is the only tool that can carry a design across frames and views, its cost is roughly 6 gen above skeleton per attempt rather than a flat 4x saving, and its genuine risk is review time with no cross-view gate. One test would change my mind: 3-4 worn items on one 4-view, 92 px, 10-frame sheet, each paired with a same-item skeleton control, scored blind on the **delivered layers** (not raw G2), with reviewed-cell count and minutes recorded; if the sheet wins >=6 of 8 blind pairs, keeps palette distance at or under the skeleton arm, and cuts review minutes per accepted item-move, the default is earned. Without the control arm, the pilot should stay a pilot.
