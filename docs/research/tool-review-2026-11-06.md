# External sprite tool review

November 6, 2026. Three researchers grouped related sources, reproduced their useful ideas on retained PixelDoll artwork, and ranked them against the [October 27 method](paperdoll-repos-review-2026-10-27.md). A fourth researcher and the coordinator cross-checked the results against PixelDoll's implementation and tests. The spikes use source-level, vendor-reported, measured, and inferred evidence separately.

## Evidence

- [Layering, slots, palettes, and runtimes](tool-spikes-2026-11-06-layering.md) covers Paper Doll Studio, Willyxz and its WebGL demo, Fantasy Character Generator, and Ultima. In the Unity demo, randomizing an outfit, selecting the bow, moving, and attacking kept equipment on the authored body. A whole-body stencil on PixelDoll's own frames retained only 53.3% of the helmet, 24.8% of the cape, and 7.4% of the sword. Equipment often needs pixels beyond the body silhouette. Exact recolor still works without an indexed texture format, and the tested RGBA index sheets were larger than the source layers.
- [Identity, frame repair, and loop continuity](tool-spikes-2026-11-06-generation.md) covers AutoSprite Wardrobe, Civitai metadata, and ComfyUI's source and sample assets. AutoSprite's walkthrough sets a dressed pose as a new base and generates another walk. Its documented output is a complete dressed sheet. ComfyUI's fixed-frame structure is closer to PixelDoll's repair route. Tight dilation alone recovered 88.8–93.2% of a deliberately clipped helmet and nothing when the item was entirely missing. Adding verified adjacent frames helped in those examples, with only 96.2% coverage for one difficult cape case. The loop analysis found a high relative seam score on a nearly static clip with little absolute change.
- [Motion, framing, and sheet masking](tool-spikes-2026-11-06-motion.md) covers the local masking experiments, Lysle Skeleton Tool, both walkthroughs, and PixelSprite FX. The masked sheet example preserved all 10,860 visible protected pixels in the target cells. A rigid translated standing item was sometimes competitive on helmets and performed poorly on demanding cape poses. Lysle's preauthored attachments and paid body redrawing serve different purposes. Its sample picker was usable without a key. PixelSprite FX's source fits an orthographic envelope over the sampled animation. The Codex browser reached its renderer, but the file chooser could not attach an original synthetic model, so an actual render and atlas were unverified.
- [Chroma extraction and pixel ownership](tool-spikes-2026-11-06-chroma-and-occlusion.md) documents general lessons from a separate workflow. An independent synthetic probe reproduced 12 skin-colored repaint pixels passing the key-preservation check because they were classified as foreground. A trustworthy stencil removed all 12 while retaining the entire 126-pixel garment. The current alpha-based front/back split also cannot distinguish two overlapping hair pixels with different intended depths.

## Changes selected after review

1. **Fit eligible equipment repairs inside Tier 1.** The common 80 px case with eight target frames and one protected reference used a 320 × 240 canvas. A 3 × 3 packing sends the same cells in the same order on a 240 × 240 canvas. This changes the required subscription from Tier 2 to Tier 1 and the estimate from 15 to 10 generations. For an existing supported layout, use the alternate only if it fits Tier 1. The shared estimate and request builder use the same layout, and extraction reads the submitted dimensions and mask. The draft evaluator also checks the provider returned exactly one grid before decoding it. The request version advances so interrupted work with a different payload stops for review instead of silently submitting a differently hashed paid request. The rule also conservatively stops interrupted workflows whose inputs happen to be unaffected. Live provider output on the new layout is unverified. The documented prices for some narrow Tier 2 canvases disagree with our area approximation, so the change leaves existing Tier 2 packing outside the experiment. [PixelLab Inpaint v3 limits and pricing](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/inpaint-v3#limitations)
2. **Explain equipment that disappears during a move.** Preview now shows a count beside the playback controls. Opening it names the actual rendered move, facing, and missing equipment. Saved items open their details in that context, where any generation has its own explicit price and action. A draft awaiting review can be identified by name. The indicator uses the same coverage result as rendering, including incomplete playlists, authored transparency, and the separate pending status.

## Live agent-mode check

The starting character was an editable copy of the bundled Adventurer. The single live request was one `edit-animation-v2` batch with three standing views for a fitted doublet. PixelLab recorded **$0.08 and zero subscription generations** in the result, matching the cash balance delta. The three source views passed the saved gates with a minimum key retention of 0.979, zero stray pixels, zero clipped pixels, and no rerolls. The west view was mirrored. Recoloring the leather left the metal at its original selection.

The 456-entry PNG + JSON archive parsed locally. The isolated garment sheet had 268, 138, 289, and 138 opaque pixels across the four standing rows, zero soft-alpha pixels, and an exact west mirror. The Walk and Cross punch item sheets were transparent, as the export warning stated. Reopening the same generated item after the interface change showed the missing frames for Walk and linked to its priced item details. Returning to Default cleared the indicator.

Automatic approval review rejected a separate paid headwear batch because it required explicit approval for that batch. The remaining work used stored artwork, browser inspection, and mocked or offline tests. The budget ceiling was $25. This sample covers a standing garment, not animated fit, held-object grip, or provider quality on the compact repair layout.

## Work that needs more evidence

| Candidate | Product decision and next evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Spatial repair mask | Keep the broad mask available for a missing item or insufficient anchors. Test a narrower mask against known-good adjacent frames and the protected body. Track recovered intended pixels and false removals independently. Run an explicitly priced A/B only after offline selection. |
| Loop warnings | Measure an absolute wrap delta and its difference from typical interior transitions for actual looped moves. Inspect the same playlist for all layers and keep intentional cuts and play-once moves out of the diagnostic. Do not move the loop point automatically. |
| Rigid motion and anchors | Keep deterministic attachments in Motion Lab until qualified per slot, body, move, and direction. A shared pivot and a hand anchor are distinct. Flexible garments need shape changes and front/back depth. |
| Repair review | Compare the approved standing design and the flagged rendered cell in the existing review. Show the protected region when available. Check that a pixel-difference overlay distinguishes intended occlusion from leaked body pixels before promoting it to an automatic gate. |
| Equipment depth, slot and dye contracts | Fix item-specific depth only with a defined precedence for direction overrides and mirrored views across every runtime. Resolve mutual hides before changing hide evaluation. Extend exact ramps before considering a new indexed shader and texture format. |
