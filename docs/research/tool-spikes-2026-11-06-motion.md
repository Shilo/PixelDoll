# Motion, framing, and sprite-sheet masking spike (2026-11-06)

This spike follows the [October 27 repository review](paperdoll-repos-review-2026-10-27.md) and challenges the assumptions in the [tool review brief](tool-review-brief-2026-10-28.md). **Verified** means supported by a file, browser observation, or repeatable offline measurement. **Inferred** marks a hypothesis, interpretation, or proposed change. No paid calls were made for this spike.

## Theik and Spiken sheet dressing

**Verified, local notes.** Theik describes packing fixed-pose cells into one sheet, keeping heads and a reference panel outside the paint mask, replacing skin with cyan, then removing unchanged and cyan-looking pixels. The prompt repeats the reference design, calls out the panel location, and freezes pose, scale, framing, and heads. The related Spiken notes identify cross-call design drift and the model interpreting the canvas as a chance to draw new characters. Spiken's comparison used a separate local workflow. Its model, settings, mask, latency, and costs cannot be recovered from the supplied images and short note. The proposed local model and LoRA require another inference stack outside PixelDoll's PixelLab-only client architecture.

**Verified, local images.** All 11 PNGs were decoded and inspected side by side. Theik's five 480 × 384 images show the original body, keyed body, mask, dressed result, and extracted apparel. The layout is five columns by four rows of 96 px cells, with 18 occupied pose cells and a reference in the last two cells. The examples demonstrate 18 poses, without evidence of 18 complete animation sequences. Spiken's six PNGs show front/back sweater references, a large multi-pose preview, two comparison panels, and the 1184 × 880 input and edited sheets. The gray background of the latter pair is fully opaque. The sample is evidence of recognizable red stripes and garment pose adaptation. It provides no transparent-layer artifact or exact identity guarantee.

**Verified, offline comparison.** Pixel differences use exact RGBA except that two fully transparent pixels count as equal. Theik's conservative mask has 34,946 editable pixels. The dressed sheet changed 18,827 editable pixels and zero protected pixels in the pose cells, including preservation of all 10,860 protected visible input pixels. A further 18,432 changes are confined to the two cells that contain the reference in the delivered dressed sheet. The corresponding input artifact has those cells empty, so source-side panel preservation cannot be measured. The apparel-only result has 14,497 opaque pixels, binary alpha, and zero pixels outside the mask. The keyed body's alpha differs from the original at one coordinate. The files are one retained example, so the observation does not establish a success rate or identify the exact provider request.

**Verified with a heuristic.** Across the Spiken input/edited pair, 238,468 pixels differ by exact RGB and none differ in alpha. Excluding approximate gray-background pixels (near-neutral, red channel 100–155 in both images), the rightmost 296 px containing the reference show 78,476 exact changes among 82,655 counted pixels, but only 311 changes with a channel delta above 25. The visual reference survives in broad form and the screenshot is unsuitable for a pixel-lock claim. The source mask was unavailable.

**Verified, PixelDoll comparison.** PixelDoll already packs fixed cells with a black-masked, same-direction dressed reference and uses a prompt that identifies target/reference cells (`app/src/lib/items/items.ts:73`, `:96`; `app/src/lib/items/run.ts:148`). The runtime uses the submitted mask to constrain extraction and verifies stored mask identity (`app/src/lib/items/run.ts:329`). It uses collision-aware green, magenta, blue, or azure and a luminance ramp (`app/src/lib/image/chroma.ts:9`, `:39`, `:54`). Existing `garmentBand` extends nominal slots to preserve garments with long hems, using observed joints where available (`app/src/lib/items/garment-coverage.ts:22`). The current worn mask still opens every pixel across those rows (`app/src/lib/items/items.ts:94`; `app/src/lib/items/run.ts:158`). An exact Theik-style hard stencil already fits the downstream contract. A mask narrower than the garment's intended silhouette would also remove valid pixels because the contract is enforced during extraction.

**Verified, concrete packing limitation.** The production route plans and validates against four columns (`app/src/lib/items/items.ts:60`, `:202`; `app/src/lib/items/run.ts:154`, `:334`, `:379`, `:676`). Calling the existing `gridFits` and `gridEstimate` with explicit column counts confirms these geometry and local price estimates for one reference:

| Square cell | Target capacity, four columns | Target capacity, best legal column count |
|---|---:|---:|
| 48 px | 39 | 99 |
| 64 px | 31 | 63 |
| 80 px | 23 | 35 |
| 96 px | 19 | 24 |
| 128 px | 15 | 15 |
| 160 px | 0 | 8 |

At 80 px eight frames and one reference occupy 320 × 240 with four columns (15 estimated gens) or 240 × 240 with three (10 estimated gens). The latter also brings the request within the documented 256 px Tier 1 per-side cap. At 160 px a 3 × 3 grid fits eight frames and one reference in 480 × 480, which the local estimator prices at 25 gens on eligible plans. At 96 px four frames and one reference occupy 384 × 192 with four columns or 480 × 96 with five. The area-only estimator returns 15 and 10, respectively, but the published 15-gen prices for 512 × 64 and 512 × 128 contradict a general area-only rule. A 10-gen estimate for 480 × 96 is therefore unqualified. The local API schema requires matching image and mask dimensions and gives an overall 32–512 px range. Geometric capacity alone does not establish price or consistent rendering at the largest target counts. [PixelLab Inpaint v3 documentation](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/inpaint-v3).

## Extropia Labs Skeleton Tool

**Verified, public modules.** I downloaded and inspected the browser module dependency tree, including generation, estimation, rendering, attachment editing, JSON, PNG export, animation, and rotation. The primary body workflow places labeled skeletons in rows by direction and columns by pose. Its estimator calls PixelLab's legacy `v1/estimate-skeleton` from the browser. Its generation manager queues POSTs to Lysle's same-site skeleton or rotation endpoints with the selected PixelLab key, one or two image/keypoint references, and target keypoints. It can queue candidates across frames and retry classified failures. The upstream calls behind the same-site generation endpoints are not visible. This is pose-directed image generation, with a separate deterministic attachment workflow. [Skeleton Tool](https://lysle.net/satoshi/skeleton), [entry module](https://lysle.net/satoshi/scripts/skeleton/Main.js), [estimator](https://lysle.net/satoshi/scripts/skeleton/SkeletonEstimator.js), [generation manager](https://lysle.net/satoshi/scripts/skeleton/GenerationManager.js).

**Verified, attachments.** The editor uses per-pose 2D anchor position, depth/order, and a manually edited rotation to select an attachment sprite column. It supports a single still image or a rotation strip with 16 angles and optional second set of 16 oblique angles. The renderer extracts that pre-rendered tile and centers it on the attachment, with Canvas and SVG paths. This preserves the source pixels when copied at integer coordinates and at native scale. The code does not segment or skin the source body with a mesh, and the anchor data alone provides no geometry for fabric deformation, anatomical grip, or occlusion. PNG sheet export reads stored frame images, uses 64 px cells in N/E/S/W rows, skips the two reference columns, and can flip the other side when a lateral image is missing. Its attachment canvas helper is a separate path, so an attachment visible in the editor cannot be assumed to be a separately exported gear layer. [Attachment renderer](https://lysle.net/satoshi/scripts/skeleton/AttachmentRenderer.js), [sheet import and export](https://lysle.net/satoshi/scripts/skeleton/ImportExportPng.js).

**Verified, samples and authoring flow.** The Codex browser loaded the editor and Villager Female, displayed the missing-key notice, and offered Druid and Villager Female from Samples. Druid was also selected without entering a key or uploading a file. Parsing both publicly linked version 1 sample JSON files confirmed ten entries and nine populated images per cardinal direction. JSON records crosshair in pixels from bottom, pose names, completed-frame IDs, per-direction frame keypoints and image references, and attachment definitions. This makes poses independently editable before asking for candidates. PixelDoll already has an offline pose/candidate workflow in the dev-only Motion Lab (`app/src/ui/MotionLab.svelte:8`, `:62`), so the useful lesson is to keep that review independent of production generation. [JSON manager](https://lysle.net/satoshi/scripts/skeleton/JsonManager.js), [Druid sample](https://lysle.net/satoshi/samples/druid.json), [Villager Female sample](https://lysle.net/satoshi/samples/villager-female-1.json).

**Verified, video metadata and transcript limits.** The official short video description links directly to the Skeleton Tool. The long video describes building a character for a 2.5D game and explicitly says a PixelLab API key is required. YouTube watch metadata and oEmbed identified both videos as Extropia Labs. Signed caption endpoints returned zero bytes. The Codex browser export for the long video contained 17 sparse auto-caption fragments with no technical content, and the short video exporter returned no transcript. The claimed 100-pose throughput is the video title. The study did not establish its generation latency, cost, or manual effort from those sources. [Short demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xdz77-Ynddw), [long demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PV-oXn8_EzM).

### Offline rigid attachment challenge

**Verified, probe.** The source was the first equipment frame in each clip and direction. A deliberately simple image-only translation matcher scores the first 11 opaque rows of the first frozen body against the target body over integer shifts of ±6 px, favoring exact RGBA and then silhouette overlap. It applies that offset to the source gear at native resolution. Scores are mean gear alpha IoU across subsequent populated frames, against the existing generated gear as a comparison sample. The oracle uses the best translation against that sample and is unavailable at runtime. Existing PixelDoll `simAligned` was also calculated (`app/src/lib/image/similarity.ts:14`). Neither comparison labels the generated gear as truth. S2 has no stored joint track for these frames. Cross Punch has only the populated south row used below.

| Clip and facing | Gear | Fixed | Body-tracked | Oracle translation | Tracked RGBA match on overlap |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S2 Walk S | Helmet | .968 | .704 | .968 | .608 |
| S2 Walk E | Helmet | .936 | .912 | .936 | .882 |
| S2 Walk N | Helmet | .757 | .891 | .910 | .654 |
| S2 Cross Punch S | Helmet | .505 | .587 | .803 | .483 |
| S2 Walk S | Cape | .625 | .651 | .729 | .570 |
| S2 Walk E | Cape | .848 | .833 | .848 | .717 |
| S2 Walk N | Cape | .857 | .844 | .871 | .685 |
| S2 Cross Punch S | Cape | .330 | .342 | .417 | .257 |
| x28 direct-edit Walk S | Cloak | .542 | .531 | | .227 |
| x28 direct-edit Walk E | Cloak | .549 | .537 | | .157 |
| x28 direct-edit Walk N | Cloak | .845 | .841 | | .270 |

**Inferred.** Integer copies avoid introducing new colors or soft edges, but even the best translation cannot reproduce a south Cross Punch cape. The image-only tracker hurts two walking helmet directions, helps north, and gives unreliable extreme shifts during Cross Punch. A reliable head track and near-static bounds are necessary even for a candidate preview. A paid pilot should isolate rigid headwear, compare approved artwork on real body frames, and separate drift from correct orientation/occlusion changes. PixelDoll already trims posed rigid head-class artwork against a shifted, dilated standing mask (`app/src/lib/items/items.ts:449`); improve or validate that behavior before adding another source of truth. The older mocap retarget spike computes joint tracks and support-foot constraints (`spikes/api/mocap/retarget2.mts:1`), a separate problem from automatic bitmap rigging.

## PixelSprite FX

**Verified, implementation.** The itch.io listing embeds the 3D renderer from a public HTML iframe. I inspected the actual `app.js` served by the iframe and the standalone site. The 3D path loads assets locally using browser files/object URLs, enumerates animation clips, and renders selected time samples through Three.js and an orthographic camera. Auto-fit renders sampled frames in each selected direction into one probe target, measures the union of alpha extents, then sets a single frustum with space for padding and outline. The fit samples at most roughly 200 projections in total, so an unusual extremum can fall between samples on long clips. The separate user zoom/pan can also cause clipping. The render path shared by preview and export applies color controls, palette reduction, fixed pixel-coordinate Bayer dithering, and optional one or two outline passes. It uses nearest-neighbor enlargement and direction-row output with an optional Phaser/TexturePacker hash of fixed rects. The tool offers camera presets, including isometric, and uses selected viewpoints of the same 3D animation, so there is no cross-view design regeneration. [Itch.io listing](https://pixelspritefx.itch.io/3d-to-pixel-art), [embedded application](https://html-classic.itch.zone/html/20095899/index.html?v=1793178184), [embedded implementation](https://html-classic.itch.zone/html/20095899/app.js?v=7.0), [official guide](https://www.pixelspritefx.com/how-to-use).

**Verified, comparison and test limit.** PixelDoll already exports fixed cell rectangles, a normalized layer pivot in Phaser, and a shared world pivot for larger gear textures (`app/src/lib/export/phaser.ts:18`; `app/src/lib/export/generic.ts:148`, `:250`). The reviewed 3D atlas rect path emits frames of a rendered asset rather than separate attachable layers with common alignment metadata. No external file was uploaded and the renderer was not benchmarked on UAL1. The offline measurements here use already-generated PixelDoll sprites and do not establish the renderer's runtime speed or exact output quality. PixelSprite FX's own privacy statement describes local processing. [Privacy policy](https://pixelspritefx.com/privacy-policy).

**Inferred.** The fit rule is most valuable for motion reference generation and for detecting source clipping. Keep a common coordinate space across body, layers, and animation when previewing candidates. Bounding-box recentering per frame would remove legitimate root motion and alter held-item alignment. Deterministic palette dithering may still crawl as the surface moves across pixel coordinates. A global outline adds new opaque pixels and needs a coordinated visibility and overlap policy across all gear. Both are poor automatic defaults for editable equipment. The fixed framing already in PixelDoll should remain part of the export contract.

## Ranked changes and rejection criteria

| Rank | What to test or adopt | Effort and cost | Evidence and boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plan an alternate grid layout when four columns cannot fit or the same targets and references fit a lower estimated price tier. Derive the decoder's column count from the saved submitted width, validate the mask and reference geometry, and use the same plan in the estimate, prompt, packer, and extractor. | Medium implementation and offline tests. Optional paid provider pilot, starting with the square 80 px case. | **Verified** layout capacity and estimated tier differences. **Inferred** provider charges and rendering on alternate layouts. Keep the current layout on equal estimated cost. Prefer a square alternate for the first quality pilot. Existing queued four-column jobs should decode using their submitted dimensions. |
| 2 | Compare a spatially constrained garment mask with the existing full-width band on failed frozen frames. Define coverage from the approved design and independently checked pose, allow deliberate sleeve/hem extension, and show frame/region scope in the existing review flow. | Medium offline fixture work, then a bounded paid A/B if an unmasked artifact is reproduced. | **Verified** Theik's one-sheet protection and the local broad-band difference. **Inferred** improvement on a PixelDoll reroll. A tight mask around a truncated failure would lock in missing garment coverage. Same-direction reference and submitted mask provenance stay essential. |
| 3 | Evaluate integer-pixel standing headwear as a free preview in Motion Lab and as a diagnostic baseline against the paid output. Use trusted head/neck joints, reject unreliable frames, and require user review before considering any production replacement. | Small offline proof, medium if promoted. No paid calls for the baseline. | **Verified** the basic tracker helps some frames and hurts others. Reject it for cloth, large tilts, yaw changes, contact-heavy moves, and uncertain tracking. |
| 4 | Add fixed-envelope/clipping comparison to the existing 3D motion lab research. Compare outliers with the current root and support-foot diagnostics and use a consistent camera across candidate variants. | Small analysis using existing offline GLB tooling; live renders optional. | **Verified** shared projection strategy in PixelSprite FX and existing app pivot contracts. **Inferred** value for rigs that animate outside the cell. A production 3D editor would add substantial setup and duplicate the motion lab. |

Reject unconditional whole-project packing, a mandatory cyan key, automatically retried paid operations, and adopting third-party proxy/legacy endpoints. Keep the simpler user flow: the player chooses equipment and moves, sees the generation scope and estimate, and reviews concrete differences on the frozen character.

The offline probe used Node's built-in TypeScript stripping, the existing `pngjs` dependency, and PixelDoll's `gridFits`, `gridEstimate`, `opaqueBox`, and `simAligned`. Private media and downloaded third-party code stayed outside tracked files. The exact RGBA comparison, grid shapes, shift search, and reference-region bounds above describe how to reproduce the aggregates.
