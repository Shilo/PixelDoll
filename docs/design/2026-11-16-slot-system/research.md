## Audit of the supplied MMO spike

Keep its central insight: equipment position, item category and rendering layer are different concepts. Reject its apparent precision about the optimum count and universal coverage.

1. The stated list contains **12 types and 13 instances with two rings**, not 11 types and 12 instances. The later section's 12-type list fixes part of that inconsistency.
2. Inventory slots include stat-only positions. Rings, relics and soul crystals can matter to gameplay without producing a separate paper-doll image.
3. Character creation categories such as Hair may be absent from MMO inventories yet essential to a visual dressing tool.
4. Favicon-style source labels are not traceable evidence. Item catalogs and third-party server wikis can also differ by edition, region and patch.
5. Synonyms and independent categories are mixed: Face/Middle head/Eyes; Wrists/Bracelet; Back/Cape/Bag/Wings; Body/Shirt/Costume. Do not convert every noun into a permanent slot.
6. Overall versus Top/Bottom demonstrates a gameplay equipment conflict. It does not require importing a general compatibility matrix into PixelDoll. A full-body visual asset still needs defined extraction bands and compositing.
7. No supplied data measures commonness, screen readability, independent swapping frequency or maintenance cost. The ten/five proposal is a product judgment.

## What would change the recommendation?

- Measure which categories real PixelDoll users swap independently, not just how many games list them.
- On existing fixtures, inspect 48px and 64px masks for glasses, gloves, cuffs and neckwear across directions. Tiny details can be exaggerated; do not equate low area with uselessness.
- Offline-test one-piece swimwear against torso/leg band clipping before advertising Underwear or Full outfit presets.
- Test simultaneous cape/quiver, copied left-hand behavior, directional background splits, hair/headgear masking and canvas bounds. These tests establish support, not demand.
- Try manager tasks on actual touch hardware. Drag thresholds and hidden-category discoverability cannot be proven from source inspection.

No paid generation is required for this spike or prototype. Propose paid validation separately only if offline evidence cannot answer a specific generation question.
