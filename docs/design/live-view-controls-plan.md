# Live Views controls and jump physics

Proposed for Studio’s Top-down and Side-scroller views. Players can steer, aim an attack independently, jump, and mount or dismount while inspecting their character and equipment. Both views share the same actions and per-character animation assignments. Preview retains its timeline controls.

## Controls by input

| Input              | Move                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Aim and attack                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Jump and mount                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Touch              | A floating Move stick wherever the first finger lands on the stage (follows the finger past its rim). Side-scroller movement is horizontal.                                                              | A resting Attack stick at the lower right (the largest control). A press centers it on the finger and attacks at once along the facing; dragging aims, and the swing turns with the aim mid-attack. Release snaps it back.                            | Jump above the Attack stick (hold to jump on every landing), Sprint (toggle) at its upper left and Mount/Dismount to its left, on an arc the thumb reaches. |
| Keyboard and mouse | WASD or arrows. A/D or ←/→ in Side-scroller. Hold Shift to sprint. Direction keys take precedence over mouse travel.                                                                                     | Right-click to attack toward the pointer from the character. Hold right to repeat; a playing attack turns with the pointer from the same frame.                                                                                                       | Space to jump (hold to keep jumping). F to mount or dismount. Attack, Jump, Sprint and Mount icons dock at the stage's lower right.                         |
| Mouse alone        | Left-click a point to walk there and stop. Hold left to keep walking toward the floor under the cursor, even with a still cursor. While right is held, continue toward the last left-button destination. | Right-click or hold to attack. The character can keep walking toward the left-button destination while the cursor aims elsewhere.                                                                                                                     | Use the desktop Attack, Jump, Sprint and Mount/Dismount icons.                                                                                              |
| Keyboard alone     | Same movement keys as desktop.                                                                                                                                                                           | E attacks in the current travel direction, or the last locomotion facing while stopped. Hold E to repeat. E and F sit beside WASD on the top and home rows, the usual one-hand action and mount keys; X and M were too far from the movement fingers. | Space and F.                                                                                                                                                |

GDevelop’s 2D example uses two virtual sticks on touch and keyboard plus pointer on desktop. Supercell uses tap and aimed drag on the same control for applicable Brawl Stars gadgets. Epic’s touch layout has a dedicated Jump action. PixelDoll uses these patterns to keep aiming deliberate and Jump independently reachable. [GDevelop 2D Twin Stick Shooter](https://gdevelop.io/game-example/free/starting-twin-stick-shooter) · [Brawl Stars aimable gadgets](https://supercell.com/brawlstars/en/blog/news/new-power-brawl-pass-changes-and-a-new-starr-drop-2/) · [Epic touchscreen controls](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/developer-customizable-touchscreen-controls-in-fortnite)

### Mouse button combinations

A mouse exposes one cursor even when two buttons are held. The left button sets a bounded world-space destination, and the right button temporarily owns changes to the cursor. A fixed world target lets the character reach the point even as the camera follows. Use only the horizontal target in Side-scroller. [W3C Pointer Events, chorded buttons](https://www.w3.org/TR/pointerevents3/#chorded-button-interactions) · [Blizzard’s Diablo II controls](https://classic.battle.net/diablo2exp/basics/controls.shtml)

| Gesture                  | Result                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Left click or hold       | Press sets a destination. While held, actual pointer motion updates it. Release keeps the last destination until arrival. A click near the character stops mouse travel.                                |
| Right click or hold      | Press attacks toward the pointer and starts aim feedback. Hold to repeat after each completed attack using fresh aim. Release stops repeats. Near the actor, use the current or last locomotion facing. |
| Left first, then right   | Keep walking toward the existing destination. Move the cursor independently to aim. After right releases, a fresh pointer movement can steer again if left is still held.                               |
| Right first, then left   | The left press sets one destination at that point. Subsequent pointer motion aims until right releases. Either release order is valid.                                                                  |
| Keyboard movement active | Direction keys own travel, including when opposed keys cancel out. Cancel the stored mouse destination when keyboard movement takes over. Right-click aiming and E still work.                          |

Give the destination and current aim quiet visual cues. Start one attack per press and allow a held input to start the next only after the prior clip finishes, with a proposed minimum 200 ms between attacks. Freeze the facing for each attack. Ignore extra taps during a clip and leave the current clip to finish on release. Releasing or leaving the playfield stops repeat. Handle both press orders with individual mouse-button transitions and reconcile lost state with the `buttons` bitmask. Clear held actions and the mouse destination on focus loss, modal entry, or view changes. Preserve middle-click zoom reset and keyboard-invoked context menus. Suppress the browser menu only for a secondary mouse action claimed by the live playfield. [MDN contextmenu event](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/contextmenu_event) · [W3C Pointer Cancellation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html)

### Layout and gesture ownership

- Superseded (2026-11-23): touch moves from anywhere on the stage and attacks with a resting Attack stick at the lower right (lib/input/StickControl.svelte, a centering and following stick from the modular lib/input/joystick.ts), with Jump, Sprint and Mount on an arc around it; desktop docks its icons at the lower right. Originally: Move on the left half and Aim/Attack on the right half on touch, the twin-stick convention (GDevelop's example). Give each stick a visible affordance and a localizable hint so its purpose is clear. Place Jump near the right thumb so it can jump while the left thumb keeps moving. Group Mount/Dismount next to Jump with a distinct icon and enough separation to avoid accidental toggles. Use one reusable action-button component with two native button instances, at least 44 CSS px touch targets, and distinct pressed and disabled states. Desktop shows the same two actions as compact, icon-only Bronze buttons along the bottom, with accessible names, `aria-keyshortcuts`, and shortcut tooltips on hover and focus. Mount has a stable accessible name and a pressed state that reflects whether the character is mounted. Keep the controls clear of safe areas, panels, notices and the actor. Switch presentation on actual input changes, including on hybrid devices, and keep controls stable during a gesture. [Apple Game Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls) · [Roblox cross-platform input guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/cross-platform)
- Assign each touch a single owner from its down target until release or cancel. A finger can cross the middle without changing sticks. Button fingers do not steer. A pinch starts with both touches in the unclaimed upper playfield, outside the lower stick zones and all buttons. Keep wheel, trackpad pinch and discrete Zoom controls as available. [W3C Pointer Gestures](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures)
- Decide pointer ownership before the zoom capture handler can consume the touch. A canceled pointer releases its own control. Route a third-finger Jump or Mount press through one activation path so the existing secondary-touch click synthesis cannot fire it twice. Keep native activation for keyboard users and preserve stage focus when a pointer activates a game button.
- Expose mode-specific controls in Settings and on first entry to a live view. Jump and Mount/Dismount use native buttons that act on press and never take keyboard focus; Space, E and F stay with the stage. Scope game shortcuts to the focused live stage, respect modal and text focus, ignore browser key repeat and drive held attack repetition in the scene, and retain held movement through action button presses. Clear held input on cancel, lost capture, blur, and mode or character changes. [W3C Character Key Shortcuts](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts) · [W3C Pointer Cancellation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html)

## Animation settings and directional attack

- Add a searchable **Play mode** section to Settings home. Explain that it sets the animations the character uses in Top-down and Side-scroller. Use `PlaybackPicker` for Idle, Walk, Attack and Jump, with its existing Moves and States groups, artwork and shared Automatic icon. Persist selections by character and stable clip ID in a validated local preference document, following the Riding pose pattern. Read the latest saved document before updating and refresh open tabs after a save. Renames keep the binding. Trashed clips resolve to Automatic while absent. Permanent deletion clears the binding so a new clip cannot inherit it by reusing the ID. Support page and global reset, workspace-copy isolation, and save errors.
- Automatic selects known roles deterministically. Keep the current Idle → Standing and Walk → Idle → Standing fallback. Use known Attack and Jump IDs when the corresponding clip exists. Show which clip Automatic resolves to. With no usable Attack clip, surface the assignment or missing-facing reason. The demo’s Cross Punch is south-only, so use a separate offline fixture to validate east/west and diagonal aiming.
- Separate travel vector, aim vector, and displayed facing. On attack, resolve the available animation for the intended aim, capture that direction, and play the whole outfit and body once. Continue moving at the normal speed, then return to the current locomotion or airborne presentation. Ignore repeated attack input during the clip. Top-down resolves compass sectors against authored facings. Authored mirrors are eligible where declared. If an intended direction lacks coverage, show the nearest compatible authored direction or report that the attack is unavailable for that direction. Avoid silently turning the character to the opposite side.
- Resolve locomotion separately from the displayed action so attacking east cannot overwrite the remembered westward movement facing. Render an active attack first, then an available airborne pose, then locomotion. With no airborne art, hold an available pose during flight and resume the gait on landing.
- Side-scroller retains full screen-plane aim even though travel is horizontal. Use east/west for the body’s profile. Offer optional **Attack up** and **Attack down** clip overrides for side view, with neutral aim using the main Attack. Diagonals select a supported sector and the matching horizontal profile. Keep the last horizontal facing for near-vertical aim so tiny input changes don’t flip the sprite. A top-down north view represents a different facing, so artwork for an upward side attack needs its own clip. Aim feedback should distinguish a selected sector from the art actually available. [Shovel Knight Specter of Torment manual](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/blog/shovel-knight-specter-of-torment-instruction-manual/)

## Jump and mount simulation

- Add height and vertical velocity to the existing ground movement model. On jump from the floor, apply an upward impulse, integrate gravity with elapsed time, and clamp to the floor on descending contact. Keep horizontal control and ground-plane camera tracking throughout. Suggested tuning starts at a peak around 35% of standing body height after 220 ms, with descent gravity initially 1.5 times ascent gravity. For target height `h` and time to apex `t`, use ascent gravity `2h/t²` and launch velocity `2h/t`. Split a step that crosses the apex so the descent gravity applies at the right time. Validate the feel in both views and at different zoom levels. [GDC, Building a Better Jump](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023559/Math-for-Game-Programmers-Building) · [Godot physics introduction](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/physics_introduction.html)
- Keep the jump’s floor position separate from the actor lift. Lift the actor away from its floor point; the floor reflection fades with the lift, so no separate contact shadow is drawn. In Side-scroller, show a clear vertical gap and land on the same floor. Use standing body height for normalization so a different frame, outfit, or viewport does not change the trajectory. The simulation owns height and keeps the authored pivot stable. Sample the Jump clip by flight phase and favor in-place artwork so baked-in travel does not compound the arc. Hold an available pose when Jump has no usable art and keep gravity running during an air attack. A brief pre-landing input buffer can be tuned after the basic jump feels right. The initial world has one floor surface.
- Include the jump apex and the top of worn gear or mounted artwork in the default live-view fit, especially in short landscape. Keep the floor reflection under the floor point and fade it with the lift. Verify the painted bounds at the apex as well as the ground contact on landing.
- **Mount/Dismount** is one action, available by button and M while grounded. Reuse the current test mount, Riding pose, leg handling and mount toggle so Settings and stage state agree. Apply takeoff to the mount and rider together and freeze the mount gait until landing. Check that the fixture reads as a jump at small and large scales. Keep mount and rider aligned to the selected presentation facing during a supported attack. Where the mount or rider lacks that facing, show the missing coverage and keep the current valid pose. On mode or character change reset the jump and any destination. While a modal or hidden page pauses the scene, suspend elapsed time so the next frame cannot skip takeoff or landing.

## Build and verify

1. **Roles and playback.** Wire the shared picker and persistence. Prove role resolution, reset to Automatic, rename, deletion, sparse facings and one-shot playback using stored frames. Leave exports and generation independent from preview assignments.
2. **Actions and physics.** Add separate travel and aim inputs, a testable vertical simulation, and a single mount action shared by buttons and keyboard. Prove that moving west while attacking east keeps westward travel, that a side-view up attack uses the authored variant and that a jump returns to the floor across frame rates. Check jumping, attacking and toggling the mount in both views.
3. **Pointer and UI integration.** Implement mouse destination walking, mouse aim, and touch ownership. Verify left-click arrival during camera following, both button press and release orders, and aiming east while a westbound mouse destination remains active. Check held attacks across clip boundaries, aim changes between attacks, release and cancellation, and native menus outside the playfield. Exercise two held sticks plus a Jump tap, pinch versus aiming, lost focus, keyboard held across button activation, and keyboard-only/one-pointer operation. Use `experience-parity` and `scene-zoom` for behavior, and cover Settings search and save/reload. Check dark and light, portrait and landscape, a hybrid laptop, and real phones for thumb reach and pointer cancellation.
