# 2D character creation and wardrobe research

Researched 2026-10-22. This is comparative evidence for PixelDoll's Studio redesign, not a UI specification. Links point to the original game or tool pages and their visual examples; no game artwork or interface should be copied. **Observation** means the linked source describes or shows it. **Inference** is a proposed consequence for PixelDoll, not a claim about user testing. Sources include developer/publisher pages, official product support, the Official Terraria Wiki, and game wikis where first-party detail was unavailable. Historical community reports are labeled as such.

## Evidence matrix

| Example | Observed structure and visual reference | Transferable lesson | Reject or test carefully |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stardew Valley** | The [character creator and screenshot](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/The_Player) offer appearance choices, randomization, and separate eye/hair/pants colors. The [farmer sprite documentation](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Modding:Farmer_sprite) describes a 16 × 32 pixel actor whose pants, shirt, accessories, hair, hat, and arms occupy a defined order; it also documents frame-dependent placement and hats changing some hairstyles. [Tailoring](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Tailoring) and [dyeing](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Dyeing) add clothing and color choice later. | Show the composed sprite at every wardrobe change, retain a recognizable body, and treat overlap/occlusion as part of the product rather than a cosmetic edge case. Do not inherit the game's material costs, delayed tailoring unlock, or fixed gender selector for a free creation tool. HSV can be an advanced control, not the only way to recolor. |
| **Terraria** | The [official creation guide with desktop/mobile screenshots](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Guide:Getting_started) shows character selection followed by creation, categories for hair/eyes/skin/clothes, HSL color bars, and a preview; initial clothes may later be hidden by armor. [Social slots](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Social_Slot) let appearance override functional gear, with [three loadouts](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Loadouts) retaining corresponding vanity/dye settings. | Treat *what the player sees* as separate from the underlying body or production layer. Visible/hidden and outfit presets are meaningful controls. | **Inference:** cloning Terraria's parallel armor, social, accessory, and dye columns would bury PixelDoll's main sprite and force users to decode slots. Its [mobile guide](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Guide:Vanity) mentions double-tapping for desktop right-click behavior: do not make a hidden gesture the only way to swap/remove a layer. |
| **MapleStory / MapleStory M** | Nexon's [MapleStory Cash Shop support](https://support-maplestory.nexon.com/hc/en-us/articles/211574166-What-is-the-Cash-Shop) names beauty items for hair and face. Nexon's [MapleStory M beauty shop notes](https://forum.nexon.com/MapleStoryMGlobal/board_view?thread=2008031) describe multiple beauty categories and preview slots that show combinations before applying them. Its [Dress-Up mode notes](https://forum.nexon.com/MapleStoryMGlobal/board_view?board=2690&thread=2018204) describe separate appearance presets and a preview toggle for a transformed state. | Preview the whole outfit and its alternate state before committing. A dedicated visual layer/status can prevent confusion when a character's displayed look differs from underlying equipment. | Reject its commerce and complexity: random/rotating cosmetics and separate normal/transformed state presets are game-specific. A [historical Nexon forum bug report](https://forums.maplestory.nexon.net/discussion/25941/cant-preview-cash-inventory-items) says some items could not be previewed even when wearable; this is a reminder to make *every* PixelDoll item previewable before any paid PixelLab generation or export. This forum report is anecdotal, not a current product audit. |
| **Starbound** | The [character creation guide](https://starbounder.org/Guide:Getting_Started) separates species, appearance, idle personality/pose, and difficulty; starter shirt and pants become equipped items. The [player page](https://starbounder.org/Player) documents distinct functional and cosmetic head/chest/legs/back slots. The [colorization page with dye comparison](https://starbounder.org/Colorization) and [dye page](https://starbounder.org/Dyes) describe color replacement on armor/clothing. | Make body identity, item slots, and pose distinct concepts. Frame/move choice belongs next to the character preview, since a static front view cannot prove equipment stays aligned while animated. | **Inference:** mixing irreversible game mode choice into a creative appearance flow would distract from PixelDoll's goal. Avoid category names whose meaning changes with species. A [developer-forum modding guide](https://community.playstarbound.com/threads/a-guide-on-implementing-character-customization-for-custom-races.136565/) explains that the same option slots can map to different species features; this flexibility is useful internally but confusing as visible UI vocabulary. |
| **Sun Haven** | The [game's Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1432860/Sun_Haven/) advertises seven races and a later salon for appearance changes. The [character creation reference](https://sunhaven.wiki.gg/wiki/Character_Creation) enumerates body, hair, eyes, face, chest clothing, and pants. [Developer patch notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1432860/announcements/detail/3657524427214023787) expanded race features, color variants, and explicit “None” options such as no wings or horns. | Put **None** beside item choices; visibility/removal is a normal wardrobe action. Let users return to the look later instead of implying their first choice is final. | **Inference:** hundreds of choices multiplied by color variants can become a browsing problem. PixelDoll starts with a small number of generated items, so show only items the user has made. Add search/favorites only if a real library warrants them; avoid a giant unfiltered or imaginary premade catalog. |
| **RPG Maker MZ** (creator tool contrast) | Publisher pages list concrete part categories including front/rear hair, clothes, accessories and cape in the [generator pack](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1351040/RPG_Maker_MZ__Character_Generator_Pack/). The [generator expansion](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2864980/RPG_Maker_MZ__Character_Generator_Expansion_Plugin/) explicitly permits layer-order edits and produces multiple character representations (full body, side view, face, walking variants). | Export and quality checks must account for more than the attractive hero pose. Expose advanced layer order/technical status near inspection/export, not inside every casual item picker. | **Inference:** copying an asset editor's full part taxonomy or “male/female/child” library split into PixelDoll would create catalog work and false promises. PixelDoll generates on one fixed base and must keep that identity across clips/directions. |
| **Picrew** (2D dress-up tool contrast) | Picrew's [official maker anatomy](https://support.picrew.me/en/create_imagemaker/create_first) separates canvas, parts, items, layers, and an operation panel that only shows available functions. Its [layer guide](https://support.picrew.me/en/create_imagemaker/create_first_process) uses front/back hair around the body; its [linked color guide](https://support.picrew.me/en/creator_functions/color_link_settings) keeps related parts on one color ramp. | Keep the sprite visible while one part/category is active. Only show controls an item supports. Link hair or outfit palette ramps where coherence matters; allow independent overrides when intentional. | **Inference:** per-part movement/scale/rotation is useful for static portrait makers but dangerous for frame-aligned sprite sheets. PixelDoll should reserve manual offsets for repair mode with cross-frame checks, not expose arbitrary transforms as the default styling interaction. |

## Actionable synthesis for PixelDoll

1. **Center the actor, then frame it in a restrained world cue.** A floor tile, shadow, and optional background help judge silhouette and contrast at game scale. Keep a plain/checkerboard inspection mode one tap away. This is an inference from the game examples' in-world actors and PixelDoll's export purpose; the sources do **not** establish that a full walkable scene is needed. For a 48/56 px body inside a 64 px cell, present crisp integer zoom for styling and a native-scale thumbnail for legibility testing. Let oversized held weapons use their 96 px extent without clipping the stage.
2. **Use one active wardrobe category at a time.** Hair, headgear, clothing, boots, cape/back, and weapon are clearer as named categories than simultaneous game inventory columns. Each category should show thumbnails only for *available, already generated* items and a prompt to describe/generate another item. Terraria, Starbound, and RPG Maker use large inventories or premade libraries because their products already contain those assets; PixelDoll does not. Keep the composed sprite and its active outfit visible throughout. Include **None**, visibility, and item removal as ordinary actions. Distinguish the frozen base body from swappable layers in copy and affordances; replacing the base is a new character, not an appearance setting that preserves item alignment.
3. **Pair beauty with proof.** The primary preview should support direction and move switching (including a side view, back view, and an action/grip pose) before export. Display layer quality status where it affects a decision: clipping, stray pixels, occlusion, or grip-side issues should be discoverable from the outfit, then inspectable frame by frame. This follows the frame-dependent layering documented for Stardew and the multi-view output in RPG Maker; PixelDoll's own quality gates supply the exact checks.
4. **Make color cohesive and reversible.** Offer accessible swatches/ramp choices first, then exact palette editing for users who want it. A linked palette can recolor coordinated pieces, while a clear per-item override preserves intention. Show before/after on the actor. Keep two actions distinct: a text/color hint in a **generation prompt** influences a probabilistic PixelLab result and may spend credits; **recolor** substitutes exact existing palette ramps locally and immediately. Stardew/Terraria's free HSV selectors and Starbound's dyes are evidence for offering color control, but do not imply PixelDoll's generative prompt has the precision or instant effect of a local recolor.
5. **Carry the same structure to phones.** Use a persistent preview above a category picker and a bottom sheet or single scrolling panel for options. Do not shrink Terraria-style inventory grids to fit. Use explicit labeled controls for rotate, play/pause, remove, and show/hide; no hover-only or hidden double-tap actions. On touch screens, target roughly 44 CSS px for primary controls as a practical design aim; [WCAG 2.2 minimum is 24 × 24 CSS px with exceptions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum), while [Apple advises 44 × 44 pt](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/). The units are different, so this is a usability target, not a claim of direct equivalence.
6. **Protect the work path.** Selection, generation progress/cost, quality review, recolor, outfit saving, and export should be understandable as successive states of the *same character*. No game example proves a town or quest metaphor improves this pipeline. If a contextual scene is used, it should clarify scale and result, not become another navigation layer.

## Traps to reject explicitly

- **A decorative world that makes the sprite small.** At real game scale, small clothing differences disappear. The stage must retain immediate zoom and a clean transparency/contrast view. (Inference from [Stardew's 16 × 32 sprite and layered render](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Modding:Farmer_sprite).)
- **Game inventories as the information architecture.** Terraria and Starbound need stats, loot, and dye objects; PixelDoll needs layer state, generated item quality, and export readiness. Their slot density is evidence of what to simplify, not a layout template. ([Terraria](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Social_Slot), [Starbound](https://starbounder.org/Player).)
- **Choosing an outfit from a single front-facing frame.** Hats can alter hair, arms may draw over clothing, and held items can fail on another side. ([Stardew's layer/frame notes](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Modding:Farmer_sprite), [RPG Maker's multiple representations](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2864980/RPG_Maker_MZ__Character_Generator_Expansion_Plugin/).)
- **Color alone as status.** Name the active layer and show selection, visibility, progress, and quality in text/icon form. Palette swatches need labels or values; this is a design recommendation, not a claim that the games meet or fail accessibility standards.
- **Gating experimentation.** Keep preview and removal reversible. Do not import Sun Haven/Stardew salon unlocks, Terraria in-game acquisition, or MapleStory commerce into a free client-side editor. ([Sun Haven](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1432860/Sun_Haven/), [Stardew tailoring](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Tailoring), [Nexon Cash Shop](https://support-maplestory.nexon.com/hc/en-us/articles/211574166-What-is-the-Cash-Shop).)
- **A catalog that promises assets that do not exist.** Sun Haven's hundreds of premade options and RPG Maker's part packs are supplied by those products; PixelDoll creates items on demand. Use an honest empty state and generated-item thumbnails, plus a generation prompt with cost/progress/status. Treat rich category grids and instant outfit previews of ungenerated items as unverified concepts. ([Sun Haven](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1432860/Sun_Haven/), [RPG Maker pack](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1351040/RPG_Maker_MZ__Character_Generator_Pack/).)
- **Precision controls without a deterministic operation.** Do not draw a continuous “choose exact generated item color” slider if the underlying AI call cannot guarantee it. Reserve exactness for local palette-ramp recolor of an existing layer. This is a PixelDoll product constraint; the games' [HSV](https://wiki.stardewvalley.net/Dyeing) and [dye](https://starbounder.org/Colorization) systems are not evidence that a generative model can deliver exact shades.
