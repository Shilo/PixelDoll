# Immersive simulation worlds

Researched 2026-10-22. Scope: art direction for the third PixelDoll study, not implementation. Read alongside the [first direction](../design-direction.md) and [round-two research](../round-2/research-prototype-worlds.md). The user's latest priority supersedes round two's recommendation: make an inviting, dimensional game space with the beauty and restraint of Moonlit Camp and Quiet Stage. Asemulator informs the purpose of trying a character in context; its visuals are not the target. HD environment studies are allowed before a later pixel-art translation.

## What the visual evidence actually supports

**Observed** below means the image was opened and visually inspected, or the named source explicitly states the claim. **Proposed** means a design inference for PixelDoll. None of these sources establishes that its layout is usable for PixelDoll, and no reference artwork is licensed for reuse here.

The strongest references are selective, not complete blueprints. The Holodeck is conceptually minimal but its grid is visually loud. The Citadel has appealing distance and materials but a crowded training-arena structure. Bescec's room is materially quiet but feels like an object rather than an inhabited world. PixelDoll should combine spatial conviction, quiet material, and an inviting horizon in original artwork.

## Three genuinely different directions

These are original proposals, not descriptions of the reference games. Each needs a convincing ground surface, one calm actor area, subdued material grid, and room for the UI. Change enclosure, light behavior, and material as well as color.

| Direction | Spatial and material brief | Why it could work | Critique / reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Moonwell Simulacrum — recommended dark default** | An immense continuous graphite-blue plane recedes into a low cyan/lavender mist horizon under a midnight sky. Broad square seams are etched into satin mineral material; their contrast decreases with distance. A soft, broad pool of cooler floor light grounds the character. No physical well, island, rim, objects, or visible stage edge. | Closest to Camp's emotional atmosphere while supporting any character genre. Its depth comes from horizon, floor perspective, and light rather than scenic props. Dark controls can sit comfortably at the edges. | Easy to overdo as a noisy galaxy or neon synthwave grid. Keep the actor area medium-dark, not black. Reject glowing checker lines, stars behind the silhouette, reflective glare, and a dark sprite disappearing into the floor. |
| **Porcelain Dawn — open, luminous, tactile** | An expansive warm ceramic surface with very shallow relief and faint square joints. The far plane gradually lifts into a soft, distant continuous curve, disappearing into pearl, peach, and lilac air. Large diffuse light gives gentle, broad shadows. The image should read as a traversable simulation space, not a cream gradient or tabletop. | A strong tonal alternative with warmth and openness. The material gives the blank space a physical presence; a dawn horizon preserves the invitation of a landscape without plants or buildings. | Pale equipment can disappear. Preserve a midtone pocket at the actor's feet and avoid a featureless white void. Reject laboratory panels, borders, portholes, giant sun discs, and excessive pastel sweetness. A light world does not automatically prove the whole app's light theme. |
| **Obsidian Chamber — enclosed, architectural, intimate** | A vast rounded chamber whose remote wall and ceiling disappear into charcoal atmosphere. A satin black mineral floor carries sparse bronze/neutral seams with slight perspective relief. Broad concealed perimeter illumination creates a low soft horizon; the actor stands on the continuous floor, not a pedestal. Reflections are diffuse and restrained. | Gives a deliberate dressing-stage feeling and a more sheltered scale than either open horizon. Its identity comes from enclosure and controlled light, with no fantasy setting. | Can become a black product-advertisement plinth or ominous empty boss room. Give the floor visible midtones and believable depth. Reject rings around the actor, spotlight cones, floating slabs, holographic UI, doorways, polished mirror floors, and glowing seams. |

## Guardrails for the next image and composition studies

- Generate the world as an **environment plate**, with no characters, interface, text, logos, obstacles, props, statues, rails, target dummies, pathways, or floating islands. Compose the character and real UI afterward; this keeps their scale and clarity under direct control.
- A grid is a surface property: joints, low-relief cuts, or differences in roughness. It follows the ground perspective and falls away into atmosphere. A full-screen CSS grid or bright wireframe overlay would repeat the rejected flat-tool result.
- Keep the lower-middle actor region calm, with a readable contact plane and reserved space for the full 96 px equipment extent. Give the upper distance enough form/light to feel inhabited without inventing set dressing.
- Treat the world as the app's visual place. No grid, bounds, or proof toggle buttons. Technical inspection belongs in an intentional task surface when needed; it should not determine the default world's appearance or add diagnostic chrome to these concepts.
- Preserve accurate sprite rendering. The environment can be HD in this study; do not blur, relight, tint, regenerate, or proportionally distort the actual character to make it match the background. A future pixel conversion should preserve the successful composition/material hierarchy rather than merely applying a pixelation filter.
- Judge the whole frame at desktop and phone sizes: if the world reads as a generic gradient once cropped, it lacks spatial conviction; if it wins attention over the character, it has too much contrast or detail. A beautiful empty background is not sufficient evidence of a good dressing screen.

Research limitations: static reference inspection only; no game or extension was installed, no in-game navigation was tested, and no creator usability study was run. The proposed horizon, palette, camera, and material choices are design judgments. The sources were consulted for visual principles and product context, not copied artwork.
