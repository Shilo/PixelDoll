# Pixel-art prototype worlds for a quiet character stage

Researched 2026-10-22 for the second PixelDoll design round. This is visual and product research, not a request to adopt another tool's mechanics or copy its artwork. **Observed** below means I inspected the linked image or read the maker's description. **Inference** means a proposed direction for PixelDoll. The Asemulator pages and screenshots were inspected, but the downloadable Aseprite extensions were not installed or play-tested. Reference links are for review; artwork is not cleared for reuse merely because it is linked here.

The core visual lesson is the *blockout grammar*: repeatable square cells, one or two material values, simple boundaries, and a small number of test landmarks. It gives the actor a world-scale context while remaining recognizably provisional. Asemulator is still an animation-preview extension, not a generator, paper-doll compositor, or game engine; its advertised feature set is evidence about that product only. PixelDoll can borrow the clarity of its stage without presenting Asemulator-like mechanics as supported features.

### The remembered MMO training space

**Likely identification, not verified memory:** Lost Ark's Trixion fits the described class-testing space particularly well. The official image supports the circular stage/void look and official notes verify a Trixion Training Center/Grounds. Community descriptions also distinguish the **early advanced-class trial near Beatrice** from the **later Training Grounds** used to tune builds; do not collapse them into one identical flow. [A contemporary player account](https://www.reddit.com/r/LostArkEU/comments/1agaey2/first_time_experience_problem/) describes the early test, and [the game's official roadmap](https://www.playlostark.com/en-us/news/articles/august-september-2022-roadmap) documents the later build-practice purpose.

Blade & Soul is a plausible alternative: [community stream notes](https://www.reddit.com/r/bladeandsoul/comments/63ww4s/secrets_of_the_stratus_preview_part_two_stream/) report the Hongmoon Training Room and F12 access, while [an NCSoft forum discussion](https://forums.bladeandsoul.com/topic/241415-making-a-new-character-without-using-lvl-50-voucher/) describes a character entering a training room around the level-50 voucher flow. [NCSoft's artbook](https://about.ncsoft.com/artbook/en/bns/index.html) shows a Hongmoon training setting, but it does not establish that the remembered creator trial used a Trixion-like void/platform. There is insufficient evidence to identify the user's memory with certainty. The design can honor the feeling of a dedicated test space without naming either game in PixelDoll.

## Constraints for a PixelDoll stage

1. **Use genuine pixel-art construction.** Draw or tile at one native pixel grid with hard edges, nearest-neighbor integer scaling, and a coherent limited palette. A smooth CSS gradient, blurred vignette, mixed-resolution decals or fractional tile scaling would make the stage look like a UI backdrop rather than a pixel-art place. A 16 or 32 px tile module is plausible from the references, but the final size must be chosen by testing a real 48/56 px character in its 64 px cell and a 96 px held-weapon extent. Do not claim a tile-size standard from the references.
2. **Keep gear stronger than the environment.** Use low-contrast cells in the actor area, sparse terrain edges, and one clear contact/shadow cue at the pivot. Allow extra negative space for hats, capes and oversized weapons. Verify dark and light outfits, not just the current demo character. Stage tiles should not masquerade as export pixels; the existing plain/checkerboard and native-scale inspection view must remain one direct action away.
3. **Treat top-down and side view as different compositions.** A top-down set needs a floor plane and restrained height/step cues; a side-view set needs a horizon or ground plane and a small ledge. Merely swapping stage colors while leaving the actor's perspective unchanged would be incoherent. If the character has no appropriate source view or clip, show a static supported pose or an honest “unavailable” state. Do not mirror or invent diagonals as if generated, and do not imply free roaming, collision, jumps, attack states or camera modes.
4. **Make it a preview context, not the editor's navigation model.** Selection, dressing, recolor, moves, inspection and export remain direct controls. The stage can suggest a prototype world with two or three landmarks; no click-to-walk requirement, unlock path, enemies or mandatory test sequence. Only actual stored frames animate. This preserves the creator's immediacy and avoids spending PixelLab credits to stage a decorative demo.
5. **Prefer a small authored room over an entire asset kit.** A square floor rhythm, edge, step and one point of interest are enough. The references show that tilemap logic can be understood with very few motifs; adding an Asemulator-size physics/control panel or a Petixel-size route diagram would shift attention away from the outfit.

The strongest round-two direction is therefore a **quiet pixel test room**: tile-locked, visibly dimensional, and small enough that the character is the hero. A side-view or top-down variant can be explored as a *visual study* only after matching it to the actual character views and clips available in the fixture. The inspection backdrop remains the truth view for alignment and stray-pixel judgments.
