# Production game experience

Implemented on `feat/game-experience-rework`, 2026-10-23. This is the production integration of the approved [Obsidian / Porcelain Bronze direction](2026-10-22-redesign/round-4-discussion/current-design.md) and [round-six playable reference](2026-10-22-redesign/round-6-prototype/README.md). Earlier research and mockups remain decision history.

## Experience and preserved workflows

Context-menu gestures share the typed attachment in `app/src/lib/input/contextMenu.ts`. Apply `{@attach contextMenu(open)}` to a row or tile wrapper; the callback receives that element, while the caller owns the actual menu. The attachment owns gesture detection, release-click suppression and listener/timer cleanup. Character rows and item tiles use this same API without their own gesture handlers.

Character rows in the library and character picker have a sibling ⋮ control for Download ZIP and Delete character. Right-click, Shift+F10 and touch/pen long-press open the same menu without selecting the character. Deletion remains confirmed and unavailable during pending generation work; the bundled demo cannot be deleted, so its Delete stays in place, unavailable, with the reason underneath. Delete character… and Delete item… use the danger color. Deleting a character or item and canceling a running item each confirm in one short centered alert (ConfirmDialog): a question, at most a line of consequence, Cancel (focused) and the danger action; Escape cancels it without leaving the screen behind. Item menus also offer Download ZIP. Character downloads preserve the current look and saved outfits; item downloads contain that item and its fitted base body, with current colors and an editable copy. One New character menu offers Create from scratch…, Import from device… and Import from PixelLab… from both the header and a final library row styled like an unselected character row (no border) at about half its height, a full 44 px target, with its plus in the thumbnail column and its label in the name column. Import sources open separate dialogs rather than tabs.

Apply [simplicity-first design](../usability-principles.md) to changes in these workflows: keep common tasks direct, reveal necessary secondary options in context, and preserve costs, errors, accessibility and recovery. The guide defines interaction principles; this document retains the workflow and theme ownership contracts.

- **Characters:** full-screen world, actual locally saved characters and bundled demo, south-view thumbnails, selection by click, focused WASD/arrows or wheel. A second click on the selected character customizes it. The bar reads "PixelDoll | Characters" with the count; library cards show each character's facts (size, directions, moves, items). No invented demo identities or simulated saved characters.
- **Character carousel:** loaded character changes move the upright artwork between the existing center and side positions in a bounded 220 ms transition. Fast input redirects current motion rather than queuing animations. Departing actors keep their canvases until their exit finishes, without restarting their exit clock; cached artwork remains shared. Wrapped peripheral actors disappear at the edge before reentering, not across the hero. The name and Customize action stay outside the motion and follow the loaded document. Two characters exchange their existing left and center positions in either direction without duplicate actors; one character never moves on navigation. Filtering, resize, DPR changes and reduced motion settle immediately. Motion does not change wheel sensitivity, native list scrolling, the theme or the loading/failure contract.
- **Create:** the complete production CharacterForm in a full-screen scene-backed surface. Name, linked Appearance/Prompt, help, API preview/copy, Enhance/Undo, body/frame, camera, original direction/mirror controls, seed, validation, costs, results and recovery retain their real behavior.
- **Customize:** a single persistent shell with Preview, Top-down and Side-scroller. An immersive top bar holds the back action, the name menu (name, facts line, prompt with Copy prompt, rename and character actions), centered view tabs, Random look, Undo/Redo, Share, Export, Full screen and More; there is no left identity block. All equipment controls sit right on desktop; phones and portrait tablets use a fixed-height bottom sheet with the view tabs floating under the bar. View tabs remain intrinsic width.
- **Preview:** animation choice, pause/play only when the displayed move has multiple distinct timeline cells, declared facing controls, Still feedback for an unavailable selected clip/facing, and an icon-only Inspect action with its accessible name and tooltip. Static moves and still fallbacks omit playback without changing the saved pause state. Requested facing remains selected even if the chosen animation has no artwork there.
- **Live views:** continuous elapsed-time movement, camera following, touch joystick, nearest-direction animation resolver and deliberate fallbacks. Missing movement animation never disables movement. Equipment unavailable for a sequence is hidden for that presentation only, without changing the saved outfit. Deliberately sparse direction sets are normal; genuine coverage gaps have deduplicated feedback and details.
- **Inspect:** the original precise direction grid, frame stepping, zoom and checkerboard tools in a native modal. The world and equipment layout remain consistent when it closes. This is intentionally separate from the immersive, uncluttered Preview.
- **Wardrobe / Colors / Outfits / Moves:** real existing components and actions, including item details, quality notes, layer controls, shared designs, fitting, generation and recovery. Wardrobe is one continuous list of every category in `GROUPS` (no More menu) with sticky headers, a slot rail on the right that follows and drives it, Empty as the first tile and a New tile only for an empty category. Each saved item tile has a ⋮ options menu at its top-left edge, a 28 by 25 px target inside its border whose top dot is level with the top of the worn check in the top-right corner (an arrowed popover at every size that the same ⋮ toggles and Tab closes: a fixed-name Wear toggle with `aria-pressed` in its header, Details…, Try again…, Delete item…, then focus on the next tile, else the previous one, else the category heading; a press on a tile while the menu is open only closes it, and a menu closed because its ⋮ scrolled away leaves focus on that ⋮) and shows its status on the tile, missing frames included; there is no selected-item row. Item details lead with warnings and review, then Wear/Try again/Delete item…, then a Recolor link to that item's Colors section, then layering and technical details. Shared designs from other characters sit inside their category's section. Item colors live in Colors: a Character section plus one per worn item. Random is scoped: the top bar changes items and colors, a category changes to a different item with its colors, Colors changes colors only; each press is one labeled undo step. Moves keeps its tab position in live views, aria-disabled with its reason (movement picks idle or walk there); Tab reaches it so the reason is announced, and arrows step past it.
- **Item thumbnails:** saved and generating items show an item-only, transparent-bound crop. While running, a draft's first usable extracted preview fits inside the progress ring's clear center at 12px, keeping the percentage and bottom rows unobstructed. Terminal draft previews use a 40px box to leave room for status; the stage and explicit review sheet retain larger inspection artwork. Static thumbnails use native backing pixels with nearest-neighbor CSS containment. Frame selection and cropping depend on artwork changes, not playback, progress ticks or viewport measurements; the stage retains its existing pixel scaling.
- **Import, setup, rename/delete, account, Activity, API disclosure, export:** existing dialogs, state and storage implementations remain in use. Paid-request safeguards are not replaced by the presentation runtime.
- **Share preview:** Move/Direction and Size/Animated use matching equal-width, wrapping control rows. A Bronze divider separates settings from Copy PNG/Share to actions and the full-width Download action. Sizes are 1x, 2x, 3x, 4x, 6x, 8x and 10x; the download label identifies PNG or GIF and shows the actual pixel resolution. All outputs preserve frame padding and use nearest-neighbor scaling. Animated sharing and all app views repeat continuously, including moves set to play once on export. Copy PNG stays visible in either mode and copies the fixed first GIF frame when Animated is on, or the static output when off. PNG data is prepared before activation to preserve clipboard user permission. No control tooltips or permanent first-frame explanation are shown. Per-frame output is limited to 16,777,216 pixels to bound working raster memory; oversized selections show an error and can be reduced.
- **Export:** Settings/Back stays on the left and Close stays on the right in one native dialog. Format, Include dressed character sheets and Play animation once names are global preferences persisted in IndexedDB by `lib/store/export-settings.ts`. Playback checkboxes list unique canonical names across saved characters, the current character and remembered selections. Names match case-insensitively without aliases or semantic guesses; unselected names loop. Renamed/new names use their own rule, and deleting a character retains remembered names for future characters. Opening or reactivating Export refreshes metadata; atomic single-field saves merge concurrent changes, with cross-tab invalidation. Export resolves preferences only on a manifest copy, including the editable copy shipped in the ZIP; importing a file never changes global preferences. App previews and shared GIFs always loop. Combined sheets are extra PNGs for the current look and saved outfits; disabling them retains separate layers and outfit definitions. Current settings remains concise and Files included has its own disclosure. Dialog height stays stable when navigating, with reduced-motion support.

Export reports invalid saved preferences and offers an explicit reset of only those preferences, preserving characters and outfits. An unrelated character with invalid animation names is omitted from the global name catalog with an identifying warning; it does not prevent exporting the current character. Invalid current-character data remains blocking. Loading or unavailable settings never display default values as though they were saved choices.

### Item generation feedback

Generating items keep progress and an approximate countdown inside their tiles, leaving the dressing view uncluttered. All pending tiles retain the same width and height as neighboring item tiles. A percentage sits above a 24px circular progress ring aligned with the Empty icon. The bottom always reserves two single-line rows: concise time or status, then the item name. Both truncate instead of wrapping, with full details available through the tile's accessible name and menu. The estimate shares the creation sheet's budget, starts at the first submission and survives reload; extra queue delays can extend it, and overruns say Taking longer rather than promising zero time. Preparing, Queued, Paused and Checking frames replace misleading countdowns. Percentage reflects request progress, not a frame-by-frame completion guarantee.

Each draft's ⋮ menu holds status details, Activity and cancellation. Review opens explicitly in a sheet, including from notifications. Cancellation defaults focus to Keep generating and explains that used generations are not refunded and sent requests may finish and charge. It stops unsent continuation without deleting paid request evidence. Failed cancellation saves remain retryable; another tab must cancel from its generating tab.

## Full screen

The shared header offers a quiet Lucide expand/contract button immediately before More on Characters,
Preview, Top-down and Side-scroller. It stays on the bar at every width, including where Undo, Redo and Random look move off it on narrow phones. This is a global presentation action, so a stable header position
keeps entry discoverable and exit one tap away on touch devices. Putting it inside More would reduce
header density but add a second interaction to both entry and exit. It uses the existing Bronze button
recipe and a minimum 44px width, with an accessible action name and native title. From 761px through
1279px the view tabs used to stay in the character details; since the 2026-11-11 refinement they are centered
in the bar (icons only under 1000px, a floating row under the bar on phones). Native modal sheets
retain their existing Close/Cancel controls; the header follows the same modal accessibility rules as More.

Entry is optional and starts only from activation. Nothing is saved or automatically restored. The
standard Fullscreen API targets `document.documentElement`, keeping the scene, dialogs, popovers and
notifications together. Browser `fullscreenchange` events determine the icon/action, including browser-owned
exits. Pending requests cannot overlap, and failures show an error notification with a recovery suggestion.
Escape remains browser-owned; app navigation does not also handle Escape while API fullscreen is active.
No keyboard/pointer lock, orientation lock, custom shortcut, dependency or CSS imitation of fullscreen is needed.

Research and PWA handoff (2026-11-02):

- Browser/PWA chrome varies by platform. An in-app control supplies a consistent location; desktop
  [Window Controls Overlay](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window_Controls_Overlay_API)
  exposes title-bar space only for installed desktop PWAs and would add a separate layout contract.
  It does not solve browser tabs or mobile access, so it is unnecessary here.
- The [Fullscreen API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Fullscreen_API) and
  [requestFullscreen](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/requestFullscreen) require
  support/permission and transient user activation. Feature detection hides the button where unavailable
  or prohibited. Requesting `navigationUI: 'hide'` expresses the immersive preference; the browser controls
  the resulting UI. No vendor-prefixed compatibility layer is added.
- The [Fullscreen standard](https://fullscreen.spec.whatwg.org/) reserves an exit mechanism for the user.
  Its state/events are authoritative; window/screen dimensions are not used to guess fullscreen.
- Prefer manifest `display: standalone` when PWA support is added, retaining this optional game-style
  toggle. [Manifest fullscreen is independent of API fullscreen](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/display):
  the app can already occupy the screen with `fullscreenElement === null`. `exitFullscreen()` cannot
  turn that launch mode into a window. Hide the control whenever
  [`(display-mode: fullscreen)`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/display-mode)
  matches without an API fullscreen element, including browser-owned fullscreen. Watch both display-mode
  changes and API events. Standalone/window-controls-overlay launches retain the toggle when supported.
- Platform support remains uneven. [WebKit's Safari 16.4 notes](https://webkit.org/blog/13966/webkit-features-in-safari-16-4/)
  introduced unprefixed element fullscreen on macOS and iPadOS;
  [MDN's compatibility data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/Element.json)
  still identifies the iPhone limitation. A home-screen standalone app removes browser navigation UI
  without requiring this API. Real iPhone/iPad/Android and installed-PWA behavior needs device validation
  during PWA delivery; browser automation covers actual Chromium API transitions and simulated launch modes.

## Ownership

`@shilo/bronze-ui` 0.2.2 remains the owner of semantic colors, light/dark surfaces, component states, borders, focus, shadows, radii, scrollbars and transparent scrollbar tracks. The refinement's reusable pieces (rail tabs, item tiles, sticky section headers with `stickyFade`, edge fades, hidden-scrollbar rails, the top toast region and a 20px spacing step) belong to Bronze too, pending its 0.3.0 release. No GlassKit and no new theme dependency. UI design contract: [Design Contract to Preserve](../../.claude/handoffs/2026-10-23-production-parity-ui-ux-continuation.md#design-contract-to-preserve).

`app/src/studio/experience/theme.css` imports package tokens/components without reset and aliases existing production workflow variables. `layout.css` owns composition, responsive constraints, reading-gradient placement and component sizing. Native controls receive Bronze recipes rather than a second palette. Shared-theme defects must be fixed and released in the Bronze repository; workflow geometry and scene artwork stay here.

`config.ts` owns per-state background URLs, floor projection art values and code-only reflection settings. Both source backgrounds are copied unchanged from the approved reference, with hashes recorded in [Production world artwork](world-artwork.md). Shared URLs reuse browser/cache resources. Top-down and side-scroller use different projections of the same world material; no unrelated pillars or new scene theme.

`WorldStage.svelte` owns canvas/input coordination. `movement.ts` and `presentation.ts` resolve vectors, facing, body/equipment coverage and animation timing independently of UI. Production compositor, pivots, oversize layers, recoloring and saved variants remain authoritative. No world lighting, tint or filtering is applied to actor pixels. Reflection is a separate optional copy. Resize, blur, modal entry and view/character changes release input safely.

Character selection anchors the chosen carousel slot at the viewer center, with symmetric neighbor positions. The chosen character stays aligned with the caption and navigation even with only one or two characters or neighbors of different widths. An absent neighbor leaves its side empty rather than shifting the chosen character or duplicating a character. Selection order, navigation, animation timing and character loading remain unchanged.

Preview places facings in uniform slots, with the selected slot centered for odd counts and consistently left of center for even counts. Each facing's visible bare-body rest is anchored by its horizontal bounds center and bottom edge, rather than assuming the saved rig pivot is at its feet. When rest art is unavailable, the union of that facing's body poses supplies a stable anchor. Mirrored facings flip the source anchor. This display-only translation also applies to equipment, reflection, hit bounds and fitting, never to saved or exported pixels. The anchor does not follow individual animation frames or worn gear, so motion remains authored rather than being recentered each frame. It is a painted-bounds anchor, not anatomical foot detection; a jumping pose can intentionally move away from the ring.

Selected and hovered rings rise by 1/32 of their own facing's rendered standing-body painted height, capped at half the ellipse's vertical radius and rounded down to whole device pixels. This is a conservative visual heuristic for standing humans, not an anatomical ratio or pixel-art standard. Transparent canvas padding and equipment do not affect it. Only the marker moves; sprite grounding, reflections, fitting, hit bounds and exports remain unchanged. Tiny art may receive no rise. Without rest art, adding more extreme body poses can change the union-based reference height.

Preview controls remain bottom-anchored and may wrap without reducing their 44px touch targets. Their observed border-box height, not a guessed row count, is shared by the layout, actor fitting, floor ring, world horizon and offline banner. Extra rows reserve room above the controls; shrinking back to one row releases it. A sheet that no longer fits shrinks, or hides when less than its usable 200px minimum remains. The measurement never switches between sheet and side rail or changes toolbar width, avoiding resize feedback loops. Hidden controls in the live views use the single-row baseline rather than retaining Preview's wrapped reservation.

Actor fitting uses a cached union of painted bounds across the body's poses and currently visible equipment, measured around the real pivot. Transparent oversize padding must not make the hero unnecessarily small. The union stays stable through playback; full compositor frames and exported pixels are unchanged. Integer physical-pixel scale is preferred; large imported artwork can minify with nearest-neighbor sampling when necessary. `geometry.ts` owns the scene composition: the hero fills 75% of the free stage height on Customize (about 60% on Characters), its feet sit just above the playback bar in every view, and the world image is scaled uniformly with its horizon on the character's eye line and its vanishing line on the character (no stretching; the live views' sky and the side view's floor horizon use the same eye line). Phones use the bottom sheet (a side rail would leave no stage at 320×400), and compact heights such as 320×568 shrink the sheet so the hero keeps a 2x whole-pixel scale; a phone too short for a 200px sheet moves the panel beside the stage when a 200px stage fits, else keeps a 200px sheet when the stage still fits above it and otherwise hides the panel until the window grows (never a 1-199px sheet). A phone held sideways (short, side rail) lowers the playback bar to 8px above the window edge and lets the hero fill 90% of the free height, so 568×320 keeps a 2x hero. The playback bar never rises above the header and floating view tabs, and the feet always stand above it. A stage 430px wide or less moves Inspect into More so playback controls stay 44px; under 300px one 44px facing button ("Facing: South") steps through the directions, so the bar is always one row. The world grows up to 1.3x to keep its vanishing line on the character and is clamped past that, so resizing never makes it jump. Selected names and playback controls center within the world area, excluding the equipment rail; toasts do too beside a tall rail, but span the whole width in short landscape (on Customize, below the panel tabs) and over a sheet, and never reach down over the playback bar. On phones, Redo and Share (and up to 430px Random look, up to 360px Undo) remain available in More; Export and Full screen stay on the bar.

The [direct prototype parity review](production-prototype-parity.md) corrected the first integration's typography, hierarchy, scale and creator composition. The [2026-11-11 refinement](2026-11-11-ui-refinement/ui-lab-5.html) (lab round 5, default options) replaced the identity block with the top bar's name menu, the category tabs and More menu with the continuous Wardrobe list and slot rail, and the None action with an Empty first tile. Phones keep search with New and Import above the cast list; the studio uses the bottom sheet. Native dialog content scrolls while its action footer remains accessible.

`focus.ts` restores actual activation targets, including WebKit pointer behavior, and positions the menus, Share panel and API Peek inspector against their triggers with four-side collision handling and the Bronze pointer. Help tooltips use the same pointed shell with top/bottom placement. Native selects and modal sheets keep their native presentation. `appearance.js` restores the saved light/dark choice before the app initializes, including native color-scheme.

## Scene zoom

Characters and Customize Preview share one absolute scene zoom: a scale is CSS pixels per art pixel, shown as a percentage, so 100% draws every art pixel one CSS pixel wide and 400% draws it four CSS pixels wide. Physical screen pixels also depend on device pixel ratio. Each screen opens at its automatic fit, unchanged, which reads its real percentage (for example 600% on a desktop or 300% on a phone), kept exactly. The fit is the selected character's actual scale: on Characters, its thumbnail's whole-device-pixel backing, any shrink to its box and the carousel's width cap; in Customize, the lineup or actor scale. The camera multiplies that fit by the shown scale divided by the fit and scales the background, characters, reflections, floor rings and hit regions together. Captions, menus, playback and equipment controls stay fixed. Live views and Inspect retain their separate camera behavior. The New character modal is a form, not this scene.

Wheel, trackpad pinch, or touch pinch on the artwork or empty space zooms. Wheel notches, keys and buttons step through every whole multiple (100, 200, 300, 400%, ...) with even steps between them while a step would exceed 25% (125, 150, 175, 250 and 350%), plus both screens' latest fits. Both screens step through that same list, and a fit appearing or changing only adds a level, so stepping one way and back always retraces the same levels, wherever a level was chosen; stepping onto a fit returns to it. A fit right beside a ladder level (696.6% next to 700%) leaves that small step. A step at a bound stores nothing. A mouse notch is one step however large, and small trackpad scrolls add up to one. A pinch follows the fingers precisely and, released within 4% of a fit or a whole multiple, lands on the nearest one (this screen's fit on a tie) unless something else changed the zoom since; the next step from between levels goes to the nearest level that way. The minus, percentage reset and plus buttons appear whenever the shown percentage differs from this screen's fit; returning to it hides them and moves their keyboard focus to the scene. On Characters, a wheel turned while Ctrl or Cmd is held steps through the characters anywhere on the screen (a trackpad pinch also reports Ctrl, so only a physically held Control key counts), and a one-finger sideways swipe of at least 48 px steps on touch; Preview zooms with any of these wheels. With the scene itself focused, `+` or `=` zooms in, `-` zooms out, and `0` returns to the fit. Middle-clicking the artwork or empty scene does the same without activating a character. Native fields, buttons, dialogs, and popovers keep their own input behavior.

A chosen scale is shared by both screens, so it reads and draws the same size on each, and survives switching views and resizing; it is never saved in a project. Until the person zooms, each screen shows its own fit. Input runs from 100% (or a smaller fit) to a shared ceiling of twice the largest fit seen this session, which never shrinks; a chosen level is never clamped by bounds that moved after it was chosen. Above the current screen's fit, the selected bare body's painted bounding-box center moves toward the usable stage's midpoint as its height grows, normally reaching that midpoint when the body fills the usable height and remaining centered thereafter. This transition spans at least a 25% increase over fit, so a nearly full-height baseline does not jump when resizing through capacity. The usable area excludes the header and the fixed caption on Characters, or the view tabs and playback controls in Preview, as well as the side rail or bottom sheet. Oversized artwork may extend behind those controls, centered around that midpoint. At or below fit, the pivot is the body's painted ground point, not the padded frame bottom. Both policies meet continuously at fit and depend on the current scale, not the last gesture's direction. The body reference is its standing rest, or the stable union of body poses when no rest is available; equipment and individual animation frames do not move it. Characters caches this metadata with its still and maps it through the actual thumbnail size and carousel cap. Pixel scans reuse the existing immutable-image bounds cache, never the wheel or animation loop.

The background uses pixelated image sampling when magnified; its original photographic softness is still part of the source artwork. Camera translation participates in its coverage constraints; if a tight plate needs additional coverage, only the backdrop grows further, never the character. The finite plate zooms out only until it would expose an edge; past that only characters, floor rings and hit regions keep shrinking around the grounded pivot. This preserves the selected ground point but cannot also preserve the enlarged eye-line relation once the background stops. Decorative reflections may fade below the stage. At enlarged zoom levels, companions that would clip are hidden without refitting the remaining lineup. Headers and controls retain their stacking and input priority over enlarged artwork.

## Intentional differences from a mockup

The prototype's fake three-character cast becomes the user's actual library. Real equipment categories and all workflow forms remain accessible. The creator has working validation and cost/recovery states rather than decorative fields. Short phones reserve space for the actual painted sprite and permit equipment scrolling/expansion; the sprite is not enlarged through overlapping controls. Source pixels and original clip proportions take precedence over generated mockup embellishment.
